## Context

The elections of 9 June 2024 simultaneously renewed the regional parliaments and the federal parliament. Each Region began its own government formation process, with very different outcomes.

## Wallonia: the fastest formation (~5 weeks)

The Walloon government, formed by the MR and Les Engagés, was sworn in on 15 July 2024, five weeks after the elections.

**Consequences:**
- 2025 budget voted by the Walloon Parliament on 18 December 2024, before the start of the financial year

## Flanders: nearly four months

The Flemish government, formed by the N-VA, Vooruit and CD&V, was sworn in before the Flemish Parliament on 30 September 2024.

**Consequences:**
- 2025 budget voted by the Flemish Parliament on 20 December 2024, before the start of the financial year

## Brussels: twenty months of formation

The Brussels government was sworn in on 14 February 2026, twenty months after the elections. Until then, several information and formation missions followed one another, and the outgoing government handled current affairs.

**Consequences:**
- Provisional twelfths regime until the 2026 budget vote on 27 March 2026
- No new regional policy during the caretaker period
- Erosion of public action capacity

## Explanatory factors

The length of the Brussels formation is explained by several structural factors:

- **Linguistic balances**: the special law requires representation of both language groups in the regional government
- **Political fragmentation**: the high number of parties in the Brussels Parliament makes coalitions arithmetically and politically complex
- **Community issues**: some parties set conditions linked to institutional reform, without direct connection to regional competences
