## 2027 taxes: lower property tax for owner-occupiers, higher income tax (17 November 2026)

On Tuesday 17 November 2026, the municipal council of Woluwe-Saint-Pierre approved its tax rates for 2027, according to La Libre. Owners who live in their home will pay the equivalent of **2,200 additional centimes** on property tax, through an allowance (see below), against **2,700** in 2025 and 2026, an 18.5% cut in the municipal share of the tax. Landlords and holders of second homes stay at 2,700 centimes. In return, the municipal surcharge on personal income tax goes back from **5.7 to 6%**, its rate before 2025; it also applies to tenants.

The municipality had raised its centimes from 2,200 to 2,700 in 2025, citing wage indexation and the pay-scale revaluation imposed by the Region, and had pledged to return to the former rate in 2027. According to a simulation by the alderman for Finance, a home with a non-indexed cadastral income of 2,000 euros would pay 298 euros less municipal property tax, while a basic tax of 10,000 euros would mean 30 euros more municipal income tax.

The revenue shortfall is estimated at **3.5 million euros a year**. The board of aldermen intends to offset it with operating savings to balance the 2027 budget, expected in December: not replacing some retirements and certain sickness absences, handing parking enforcement to parking.brussels and transferring the Eddy Merckx school centre to Wallonie-Bruxelles Enseignement. With the equivalent of 2,200 centimes, Woluwe-Saint-Pierre would have the lowest rate of the nineteen municipalities for owner-occupiers in 2027, ahead of Auderghem (2,440), according to La Libre's ranking.

La DH of Monday 23 November 2026 explains the mechanism: the municipality keeps 2,700 additional centimes for all owners, and an automatic allowance, from tax year 2027, brings owner-occupiers to the equivalent of 2,200 centimes. According to the paper, Brussels Fiscality, the regional tax administration, does not allow different rates for owner-occupiers and other owners; the allowance achieves the same result. The municipality is taking over the scheme launched by the City of Brussels in 2026 and will contribute, under a key to be agreed with the City, to financing the software that automates the allowance. La DH and La Libre belong to the same press group.

## A massive electoral result

Woluwe-Saint-Pierre is a residential municipality of approximately 42,000 inhabitants. In the 2024 elections, the joint list Cerexhe (Les Engagés) - Bertrand (MR) achieved a massive result of **61.7%** (+30 points), winning 24 of 35 seats. Competencies are split 50-50 between Engagés and MR.

## Transparency: 4 out of 6 criteria

The **budget**, council **minutes** and agenda are published online, sessions are broadcast via **livestream** on YouTube, and the **mandate registry** is accessible via the transparency page. However, no **Open Data** portal and no **participatory budget** despite earlier commitments. Only advisory councils exist.

## Absence of structured citizen participation

Woluwe-Saint-Pierre is one of the few Brussels municipalities without a participatory budget or citizen participation platform, despite commitments made in 2019.

## Parking: transfer to Parking Brussels

From **1 March 2026**, Woluwe-Saint-Pierre hands over parking management to **Parking Brussels** (regional agency). The free parking period drops from 30 to **15 minutes** (exception: Place Dumon, 45 minutes). **Scancar** vehicles will gradually replace stewards for enforcement. Fines will be sent by post. The municipality expects a **30%** increase in parking revenue.

## Squat at 182 Avenue de Tervueren: tensions and legal proceedings (March-April 2026)

Since **25 February 2026**, a vacant building at 182 Avenue de Tervueren (former Tirlemontoise) has been occupied by around **45 people** — women from the DRC accompanied by around fifteen children aged 1 to 11. The owner, who has a housing permit in progress, filed an eviction request with the justice of the peace. The council session of **31 March 2026**, attended by around a hundred residents and an interpellation signed by 130 people, saw tense exchanges: the Ecolo, DéFI and PS groups called for municipal mediation, while Mayor Benoît Cerexhe (Les Engagés) called the situation "inhumane" but declined mediation absent the owner's agreement; the Ecolo-Groen councillors walked out of the session. A hearing was held on 1 April 2026 before the justice of the peace, with the ruling reserved; the outcome of the proceedings could not be confirmed from available sources.
