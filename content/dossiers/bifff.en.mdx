dprCommitment: "The 2026-2029 Brussels government agreement does not name the BIFFF. Culture is handled transversally (reading, dance, accessibility, seniors), with no line dedicated to film festivals. The visit.brussels cuts (−64% by 2029) and the removal of the \"Image de Bruxelles\" envelope, denounced in the RAB/BKO appeal, affect the festival indirectly, with no exceptional measure foreseen. According to a ministerial answer to the Brussels Parliament, the BIFFF received €120,000 a year for the image of Brussels in 2024 and in 2025; no 2026 amount has been found. At municipal level, the City of Brussels adopted on 20 April 2026 (item 62) a 2026-2028 convention providing for €250,000 a year, revisable each year; only 2026 is funded by the decree."

It is held at Brussels Expo (Palais 10, on the Heysel plateau). The 45th edition is announced from 25 March to 10 April 2027, with no venue communicated as of 22 September 2026."

  - q: "Who funds the BIFFF?"
    a: "Several levels of government. The Fédération Wallonie-Bruxelles pays it €120,000 a year through the Centre du Cinéma. In April 2026 the City of Brussels adopted a 2026-2028 convention providing for €250,000 a year, revisable each year. In 2024 and 2025, the Region granted it €120,000 a year to promote the image of Brussels, and the COCOF €12,000 for culture. The Loterie Nationale is among its partners. No Dutch-speaking subsidy could be documented."

  - q: "Is the BIFFF's funding fragile?"
    a: "Two channels are framed by written texts, but both are renewed by decision. At the FWB, support for 2027-2030 goes through a new session and renewal is not automatic. The City's convention only funds 2026: the 2027 and 2028 amounts must be applied for again. The Region's and COCOF's subsidies are annual and optional, amid regional budget cuts. The festival's managers have been alerting public authorities since 2022 to the gap between constant subsidies and rising costs."

> *« On navigue à vue. On n'a aucune vue sur le suivi qui est fait, on n'a pas encore l'orientation claire et définie de la région sur le secteur culturel. On voit que la culture est dans le viseur politique actuellement. »*
>
> *(English translation: "We're flying blind. We have no visibility on the follow-up being done, we still don't have the Region's clear and defined orientation on the cultural sector. We can see that culture is currently in the political crosshairs.")*
>
> — Jonathan Lenaerts, BIFFF communications manager, RTBF, 16 March 2026.

The Brussels International Fantastic Film Festival (BIFFF) opened its 44th edition on 3 April 2026 at Brussels Expo, Palais 10, and closed on 18 April. Reconfigured format: **a single screening room mobilised** instead of a multi-hall set-up, and the **time window extended to 16 consecutive days** (from 3 to 18 April) in order to cover three weekends of higher attendance. The management explicitly described this choice as "cautious". The underlying economic logic was clear: lower the fixed costs (rent, technical, security of a single hall) while extending the operating window to optimise cumulative attendance — an economic re-engineering under constraint rather than a pure shrinkage. The edition's theme was *La vie est belle* (*Life Is Beautiful*). **According to the organisers, the 44th edition closed on Saturday 18 April with 60,000 spectators, a 30% rise and 12,000 more people than the previous edition.** More than forty years after its founding, the festival has become a textbook case of Brussels institutional fragmentation — and its economic model is being redeployed in real time under the effect of a regional austerity trajectory that has already claimed more radical victims.

## A private institution, a public dependence

The BIFFF is run by **two separate ASBLs** sharing the same address at rue de la Comtesse de Flandre 8 in Laeken: **BIFFF ASBL** (BCE 0431.844.988, founded 8 July 1986) and **Peymey Diffusion ASBL** (BCE 0419.261.714, founded 23 February 1979, classified as "specialised sound, image and lighting services"). Peymey Diffusion is presented by the BIFFF as the "parent" structure. Its 2024 revenue reaches **€985,337** for **10 FTE** — a figure that constitutes a *floor* of the festival's total cost, as the real aggregated budget is not published.

The festival was created in 1983 by **five founders** who met in spring 1982 at the Cheval Blanc restaurant on rue Haute: Annie Bozzo, Gigi Étienne, Freddy Bozzo, Georges Delmote and Guy Delmote. The BIFFF ASBL board of directors in 2026 still includes **four of the five founders** — Georges Delmote (chair), Freddy Bozzo and Guy Delmote (vice-chairs), Annie Bozzo (treasurer). Gigi Étienne is no longer on the board. Forty-three years later, no public succession plan has been announced. Operational management is handled by a salaried team (Jonathan Lenaerts on communications, Chris Orgelt on programming, Dianne Leenders on finance, Youssef Seniora on event coordination).

## Five addresses, four levels of government

The BIFFF's spatial trajectory is, in itself, a sensitive mapping of Brussels institutional fragmentation.

| Period | Venue | Level of government | Reason for leaving |
|--------|-------|---------------------|--------------------|
| 1983-2006 | **Passage 44** (Bd du Jardin Botanique) | Private | Heavy building renovation |
| 2007-2012 | **Tour & Taxis** | Private (Extensa, Ackermans & van Haaren BEL20) | "Too big, poorly adapted, almost made it lose its soul" (RTBF); site under Region-City-private PPAS negotiation throughout the period |
| 2013-2019 | **Bozar — Palais des Beaux-Arts** | **Federal** (Régie des Bâtiments) | Fire of 18 January 2021 (roofs, Henry Le Bœuf Hall, exhibition rooms, great organ) — multi-year restoration |
| 2020-2021 | Cancelled / online | — | COVID-19 |
| 2022-2026 | **Brussels Expo, Palais 10** (Heysel, Laeken) | **Municipal** (Brussels Expo ASBL, controlled by the City of Brussels) | Lease announced "for at least five years" in 2022; no public source on what follows. Venue of the 45th edition (2027) not announced as of 22 September 2026 |

Each move was triggered by an event *beyond the festival's control*, and each successive host belonged to a different level of government. **Private → private → federal → municipal**: there is no institutional continuity. A failure at one level (the 2021 Bozar fire, federal infrastructure) forced the BIFFF into a rescue at another level (Heysel, a municipal asset).

### The 2026-2028 municipal convention (adopted on 20 April 2026)

> **The Municipal Council adopted the convention on Monday 20 April 2026 (item 62), in the vote on all items of the session. It remains subject to regional oversight (resolutive condition, article 6). As of 22 September 2026, no decision to suspend or annul it has been found.**

On Monday 20 April 2026, the first day of dismantling of the 44th edition, the Municipal Council of the City of Brussels adopted (item 62) a **partnership convention** with the Peymey Diffusion ASBL covering editions **2026, 2027 and 2028**. According to the full record of the session, the item was discussed and then adopted in the roll-call vote on all items; the detail of the vote on this item is not known, as the 2026 minutes are not published. The draft decree, prepared by the Beaux-Arts department, cites article 117 of the New Municipal Law and the law of 14 November 1983 on the control of the granting and use of certain subsidies.

Article 3 of the convention provides for an annual subsidy totalling **€250,000**, split as follows:
- **€150,000** charged to the **Burgomaster's Cabinet**;
- **€100,000** charged to the **Alderwomanship for Culture**.

The draft decree charges the expenditure to articles 76201/33202 and 77210/33202 of the 2026 ordinary budget. The convention does not link each article to a cabinet.

The convention frames the three editions, but only 2026 is funded: the decree commits only appropriations from the 2026 ordinary budget. Article 3 states that the support "will be reviewed annually". For 2027 and 2028, the organiser must apply for a new subsidy; if the City grants it, the conditions of the convention are renewed, except possibly the amount, which "may differ from one year to the next". Without a new application accepted by the City, nothing is renewed. The convention remains subject to a **resolutive condition** (article 6): it lapses if the regional oversight authority suspends or annuls the decision.

**Non-cash support also documented.** The annex to the convention, entitled "Logistical support from the City", details in a public document the material resources mobilised alongside the cash: road-works services (3 trucks, 2 to 3 vans and 12 to 18 staff per day of set-up/tear-down), OPP joinery service (set-up of 240 partition panels with 15 tall and 15 low display cases, mobilising up to 24 people across 3 days of set-up and 2 days of tear-down), transport service (EPV), and provision of the urban display network (25 JCDecaux 2 m² posters over two weeks, City play dynamic screens, 23 Rentapub street-light poles with 46 banners). No amount is costed for this support.

**In return** (article 2), the organiser commits to displaying the City's logo, mentioning support in all promotional communication, providing a communication insert in the programme, ensuring site security, and making tickets available to the cabinets based on a guest list. Standard obligations for a municipal partnership, with no editorial clause or oversight on the programming.

The convention names as signatories, **for the City**, **Nawal Ben Hamou (Alderwoman for Culture)** and **Emilie Dupont (Secretary of the City)**, for the College; **for Peymey Diffusion**, **Youssef Senoria (Administrator delegated to daily management)**, spelling of the convention (bifff.net writes "Seniora"). The published document is a draft: the date of the decision and the date of signature are left blank, and the actual signature is not documented. The management report and financial statement are due to the City by **31 May 2027**, with an obligation to reimburse (plus legal interest from 1 September) in case of non-use or diversion of funds.

**Levels of government with no documented subsidy**:
- **VGC**: no documented subsidy to the BIFFF; VGC werkingssubsidies require operation in Dutch, which the BIFFF's French-speaking structure does not provide. A ministerial answer to the Brussels Parliament (19 May 2025) nevertheless lists the VGC and the Flemish Community among the "levels of government granting subsidies", with no amount or detail;
- **VAF (Vlaams Audiovisueel Fonds)**: the "toegekende steun" database returns no BIFFF/Peymey entry;
- **Beliris**: no festival line;
- **Creative Europe MEDIA**: not verified at this stage, to be investigated via the EACEA database.

→ **No VGC or Flemish subsidy to the BIFFF could be documented.** This is the central element of the BIFFF's institutional asymmetry.

### The linguistic paradox

The festival is officially named *in English* ("Brussels International Fantastic Film Festival"), a choice this dossier reads as a way of bypassing the FR/NL divide. Its legal structure is French-speaking (statutes in French, French-speaking Brussels ASBL). Its documented public funding comes from the FWB, the Loterie Nationale, the City of Brussels, the Region and the COCOF; no Dutch-speaking subsidy could be documented. But its recruitment runs through **RAB/BKO**, the bilingual Réseau des Arts à Bruxelles / Brussels Kunstenoverleg network, and its audience is multilingual.

The strategic consequence is radical: **no political sponsor on the Dutch-speaking side** will feel obliged to defend the festival if austerity pressure hits. The English name protects the programming day-to-day, but it provides no political safety net in a crisis. This is exactly the situation Lenaerts described in March 2025 and 2026.

## Heysel: the schizophrenic situation

Since 2022, the BIFFF has been hosted at **Palais 10 of Brussels Expo**, an **ASBL founded in 1936** whose statutes published on its official site specify that:

- **directors must be members of the Municipal Council of the City of Brussels** (or specifically authorised by it),
- candidates are **presented by the College of the Burgomaster and Aldermen**,
- the **Burgomaster of Brussels is a member by right**,
- mandates last 6 years, renewable.

Brussels Expo manages the Heysel palaces conceded by the City. It is, in practice, **a municipal operator**.

**Philippe Close**, Burgomaster of Brussels since 20 July 2017 (succeeding Mayeur post-Samusocial), reconfirmed after the October 2024 municipal elections in a PS-Vooruit / MR / Engagés coalition, **has chaired Brussels Expo since 2013**. For any BIFFF event taking place at Heysel, Philippe Close potentially combines five roles:
- ASBL chair (the lessor, Brussels Expo),
- Burgomaster (the permit authority for events in public space, art. 119/135 NLC),
- head of administrative police (Brussels-Ixelles Police Zone),
- head of the municipal executive (ultimate grantor of the palaces),
- **direct funder**: article 3 of the convention adopted on 20 April 2026 charges **€150,000 out of €250,000** (60%) to the Burgomaster's Cabinet and €100,000 (40%) to the Alderwomanship for Culture. Already in 2025, the €100,000 subsidy on the agenda of 23 June was among the Burgomaster's items.

Medor (No. 36, Autumn 2024) describes this configuration as a "schizophrenic situation" between municipal interests and organisational interests, and documents:
- the **2017-2018 merger** of the FIB, FSB and Brussels Summer Festival ASBLs into Brussels Expo, a direct consequence of post-Samusocial reforms,
- a **leadership crisis in January 2023** (CEO Denis Delforge and two top managers threaten to resign, mediator appointed, strike notice),
- **"unacceptable safety risks"** documented on the palaces in 2023,
- an estimated COVID revenue loss of **€80 million**.

### NEO resurfaces

The 2026-2029 Brussels government agreement revives **NEO** on the Heysel plateau. The project, long dead after the burial of Eurostadium in 2018, is repivoted around a **CONFEX** model (CONference + EXhibition) — a new congress centre integrated with the existing halls, supplemented by a **Parc des Sports** on the south of the plateau (athletics, hockey, rugby) and a transformation of the large empty car parks into housing/retail/leisure. **Governance is now regional**, with funding planned via existing NEO structures plus external and private finance.

The agreement explicitly guarantees the project's coexistence "with iconic monuments such as the Atomium or the exhibition halls" — so the palaces are not being demolished. But the BIFFF finds itself **a tenant in a multi-year construction zone**, on buildings whose safety has been flagged, while no public source reports what follows the lease announced in 2022 "for at least five years".

Sources: brussels-expo.com/en/transparence; medor.coop (No. 36); rtbf.be (NEO); rtbf.be (2024 municipal elections).

## BIFFF in the 2026 Brussels austerity: compression under constraint

The BIFFF 2026 opened precisely as the Brussels government agreement of **12 February 2026** was deploying its savings plan (~€1 billion over four years, return to balance in 2029).

The most visible cutting instrument affects **visit.brussels**, the regional image and tourism agency whose "Be Brussels" logo the BIFFF displays as a partner. DG Patrick Bontinck confirmed to *Belgeo* (26 March 2026):

> *« La Région bruxelloise réduit nos financements de 5,7 millions d'euros en 2026, sur un budget total de 22 millions d'euros. »*
>
> *(English translation: "The Brussels Region is cutting our funding by 5.7 million euros in 2026, out of a total budget of 22 million euros.")*

Confirmed trajectory: **€22M → €8M by 2029**, i.e. **−64%**. **Up to 90 jobs out of 160 are threatened**. The **direct cultural victims** already confirmed in March 2026:

- **BD Comic Strip Festival 2026 — cancelled**
- **Festival of the Iris 2026** — reduced to a single concert
- **J'aime les sciences** (October 2026) — cancelled
- all event promotion frozen, with the exception of Pride.

Unlike the festivals that have already fallen (BD, Iris, J'aime les sciences), the BIFFF was not hit by a direct cut or cancellation. Its 2026 strategy — concentrating programming on a single hall and extending the window to 16 days to capture three peak weekends — was a deliberate economic re-engineering rather than an imposed amputation. The final tally announced by the organisers (60,000 spectators, +30%) indicates that the festival absorbed the pressure without brushing the breaking point: redeploying the model produced audience growth, not amputation. But exposure to the two threats identified by the RAB/BKO (visit.brussels, Image de Bruxelles) remains, with a larger footprint than the festivals already fallen (60,000 spectators in 2026, +30% vs previous edition, international visibility, founding member of the Méliès International Festivals Federation, FIAPF accreditation). Its visibility makes it a **privileged barometer**: what the BIFFF had to reconfigure in order to keep growing says, more precisely than a regional budget, what Brussels austerity imposes on a well-equipped intermediate cultural operator.

The bilingual **RAB/BKO** network published on 7 March 2026 a call co-signed by 120+ institutions not to sacrifice the cultural sector, targeting three threats in the agreement: removal of the "Image de Bruxelles" envelope, ACS reform (which threatens sustainable employment), visit.brussels cuts. The BIFFF cumulates at least two of the three. According to a ministerial answer in the Finance Committee of the Brussels Parliament (19 May 2025), it received €120,000 a year for promoting the image of Brussels in 2024 and in 2025. The 2026 amount has not been found.

### The BIFFF in the government agreement: a blind spot

The 2026-2029 DPR **does not mention the BIFFF**. Culture is treated transversally there (accessibility, reading, dance, seniors), with no line dedicated to film festivals. No exception, no targeted measure. The festival is neither protected, nor named, nor exempted.

### The sequence of alerts (2022-2026)

Lenaerts stated explicitly to *L'Avenir* on 27 April 2025: **"We've been alerting public authorities since 2022."** The equation "constant subsidies + rising costs" tipped after the post-Bozar move and post-COVID inflation of rights, insurance and rental costs at Palais 10. The public statements of March 2025 and March 2026 are therefore not a cyclical reaction to a new crisis: they are the culmination of three years of structural compression.

## 44th edition final tally — the re-engineering validated by attendance

At the festival's close, on Saturday 18 April 2026, the organisers announced **60,000 spectators**, which, according to their statement, is a **30% rise** and **12,000 more people** than the previous edition. The two figures do not match exactly (12,000 more people on a base of 48,000 makes 25%), and Écran Noir writes "almost 30%". This result was achieved in a compact format: one hall, 16 days, three weekends covered.

The BGM reading is direct: deliberate yield management — concentrating the offer on a single hall to compress fixed costs, extending the time window to capture three weekends — did not drive the audience away. It brought them in. The hypothesis of "imposed shrinkage" is therefore refuted by the numbers: re-engineering under constraint works *both* as a survival strategy *and* as an engine of audience growth. For other Brussels intermediate cultural operators facing the same austerity trajectory, the precedent is instructive — but it rests on an operational re-engineering capacity and an already-established reputation that younger or more local festivals do not necessarily possess.

The prize list has consecrated an international cinema, with the **Golden Raven (Corbeau d'Or)** awarded to *Never After Dark* by Dave Boyle (Japan). The Silver Ravens went to *Tristes Tropiques* by Hoon-jung Park (South Korea) and *Nirvanna The Band The Show The Movie* by Matt Johnson (Canada). The European competition rewarded *Nightborn* by Hanna Bergholm (Finland/Lithuania/France/UK). Organisers commented: *"This edition once again confirms the richness and diversity of fantastic cinema"*.

This attendance level also turns ticketing into a **significant revenue source** that the figures published as of 22 September 2026 (mainly the 2024 Peymey revenue of €985,337) do not reflect — the 2026 report due to the City by 31 May 2027 will be the first public document to aggregate it with the subsidies.

This tally does not extinguish any of the risks identified above: what follows the Brussels Expo lease is not documented, the FWB decision on 2027-2030 support was not published as of 22 September 2026, the 2027 and 2028 municipal amounts must be applied for again, and exposure to the regional channel remains. It shifts the frame: the BIFFF is no longer solely a *defensive* barometer of Brussels cultural austerity. It also becomes an *offensive* textbook case of a response to pressure, to be confirmed or disproven over editions 45 (from 25 March to 10 April 2027) and 46 (2028), framed by the municipal convention adopted on 20 April 2026.

## The structural asymmetry of the lasagne

The BIFFF provides an unusually clear textbook case — and the municipal convention of 20 April 2026 alters the reading:

- The **FWB Centre du Cinéma line (€120,000/year)** falls under the support for audiovisual operators of the Commission du Cinéma, which meets every two years. The 2026 session (application deadline 11 May 2026) covers 2027-2028 or 2027-2030, with hearings scheduled for September 2026. The Commission then has five months to submit its opinion to the competent minister, who decides whether to follow it. Renewal "is not automatic", and the subsidy granted may be lower than the amount requested. The provisional festival budget is estimated at **€1,028,000/year** for 2027-2030, "subject to change". No public source confirms that the BIFFF submitted an application. If it is accepted, this is a funding floor over two or four years.
- The **City of Brussels line** has changed form. In 2025, a one-off €100,000 subsidy for the BIFFF was on the agenda of the council of 23 June 2025 (item 8). The convention adopted on 20 April 2026 sets €250,000 for 2026 and frames the 2027 and 2028 editions: the support "will be reviewed annually", and each of those two years requires a new application accepted by the City (art. 3). It remains subject to regional oversight (art. 6). The *protocol* of support is now written and public, but the financial commitment covers only 2026. A separate €10,000 subsidy for the festival's communication was placed on the agenda of the council of 29 June 2026 (item 80, Tourism); its adoption could not be verified, as the record of that session was not published as of 22 September 2026.
- Conversely, the **regional** channels remain *annual and optional*. According to a ministerial answer to the Brussels Parliament (19 May 2025), in 2024 and in 2025 the BIFFF received a €120,000 regional subsidy for promoting the image of Brussels and €12,000 from the COCOF for culture. No 2026 amount has been found. The "Image de Bruxelles" envelope and visit.brussels are among the lines that the 2026 austerity agreement has chosen to cut first.

→ The institutional lasagne rests on **two channels framed by written texts (Community FWB, Municipal City)**, both of which are renewed by decision: the FWB for 2027-2030, the City year by year. It remains **exposed in the regional middle**. In 2026, documented public cash funding reaches **€370,000** (€120,000 FWB and €250,000 City), without the 2026 regional and COCOF amounts, which were not found. This floor is firmer than earlier literature suggested, but it is secured only for 2026, and the regional zone remains the main vulnerability. Another asymmetry: the municipal convention covers three editions (2026-2028), while the FWB session covers 2027-2028 or 2027-2030; no municipal text covers the 2029 edition.

## Caveats — what remains to be dug into

This version of the dossier relies on verified primary sources (FWB funding decisions, Brussels Expo statutes, public statements from the BIFFF team, Belgeo / RABKO / regional agreement communiqués, the full text of the City-Peymey Diffusion convention published on the agenda of the Municipal Council of 20 April 2026, the full record of that session and a ministerial answer of 19 May 2025 to the Brussels Parliament). Several grey areas remain to be actively investigated:

1. **Real total festival budget**. The €985,337 of 2024 revenue for Peymey Diffusion is a *floor*, not a total. An activity report or a direct interview would be needed to estimate the real production (rights, venues, travel, guests). The 2026 report is due to the City by 31 May 2027, which sets a public documentary deadline.
2. **Articulation between Peymey and BIFFF ASBL**. The City-Peymey Diffusion 2026-2028 convention contracts Peymey Diffusion as the legal recipient of the municipal subsidy — a clue to the operational architecture. Still to clarify: who pays salaries, who receives the FWB subsidy, who contracts with Brussels Expo. The full split is not public.
3. **Brussels Expo rent (Palais 10)** — *partially closed caveat*. The "Logistical support from the City" annex to the 20 April 2026 convention details the in-kind municipal support (road works, OPP joinery, EPV transport, JCDecaux and Rentapub display) — documenting one layer of in-kind support. The Palais 10 rent itself, however, is still not published: market rate, subsidised, or partly included in the City's concession to Brussels Expo?
4. **Municipal and regional subsidies**: *partially closed caveat*. The convention adopted on 20 April 2026 sets €250,000 for 2026 (€150,000 Burgomaster's Cabinet, €100,000 Alderwomanship for Culture); the 2027 and 2028 amounts depend on new applications. Direct regional subsidy is documented for 2024 and 2025 (€120,000 a year for the image of Brussels, and €12,000 from the COCOF), not for 2026: to be confirmed via the Brussels regional subsidy cadastre (open data).
5. **What follows the Brussels Expo lease**. This is the decisive operational uncertainty: no public source found as of 22 September 2026. The municipal convention commits the organiser to the 2026 to 2028 editions without setting a venue beyond 2026, and bifff.net announces the dates of the 45th edition (from 25 March to 10 April 2027) without a venue.
6. **Succession plan**. The 4 founders still on the board are between 65 and 75 years old (hypothesis to confirm). No public plan.
7. **Creative Europe MEDIA**. Beneficiary or not? EACEA database to be queried.
8. **Points not established as of 22 September 2026**: the detail of the vote on item 62 (the 2026 minutes are not online), the actual signature of the convention (only an unsigned draft is published), any oversight decision, the adoption of the €10,000 subsidy placed on the agenda on 29 June 2026, whether the BIFFF submitted an FWB application and its outcome, the 2026 regional and COCOF amounts.

These points do not invalidate the dossier's thread: they refine its contours and set the agenda for the next iteration.

## Why the BIFFF matters for Brussels governance

The BIFFF is not just a genre festival. It is:

- one of the **rare mid-sized private Brussels cultural operators** whose funding trajectory simultaneously crosses four levels of government,
- a **direct witness** to the effect of political transitions (Samusocial, Eurostadium/NEO, 2024-2026 formation, 2026-2029 austerity) on real cultural programming,
- a **barometer** of the resilience of Brussels intermediate cultural operators: its 2026 re-engineering (yield management: one hall, 16 days, three weekends) showed that an international-scale festival can absorb austerity pressure without breaking, with an audience announced as growing (60,000 spectators, +30% according to the organisers). With the 2026-2028 municipal convention adopted on 20 April 2026, the festival appears *better anchored* publicly than earlier literature suggested (€370,000 in cash at two levels in 2026, subject to regional oversight). The question for 2027-2028 shifts: it is no longer whether the festival "holds" in a compact edition, but whether the re-engineering is *replicable* over the following editions. Three decisions remain determinative: what follows the Brussels Expo lease, FWB support for 2027-2030 and the 2027 and 2028 municipal amounts. The residual pressure lies on the regional zone (visit.brussels, Image de Bruxelles) and on structural costs (operating, insurance, rights, security), not on the demonstrated viability of the format.

Following it means following — in real time and with a very legible metric (is the festival happening? in what format? how many rooms? how many days?) — the material effect of political decisions that, elsewhere, remain abstract.
