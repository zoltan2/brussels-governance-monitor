## The countdown

On **19 December 2024**, the Constitutional Court annulled article 3, 1°, second paragraph of the Brussels ordinance of 9 June 2022 on taxi services. That provision set the conditions under which an operator holding a Flemish or Walloon licence could carry out trips on Brussels territory.

The annulment is based on **competence**, not on substance. The Court writes, in its grounds (point B.50):

> Thus, the ordinance-making legislator unilaterally adopted provisions that relate specifically to the exercise, on the territory of the Brussels-Capital Region, of taxi services operated from the territory of the Flemish Region and of the Walloon Region, and which therefore extend over the territory of more than one region. The settlement of such matters must be the subject of a cooperation agreement under article 92bis, § 2, c), of the special act of 8 August 1980.

This cooperation agreement has been mandatory **since 1993**. It has never been concluded.

The Court **maintained the effects** of the annulled provision until the agreement enters into force, "and until 30 June 2027 at the latest". This maintenance gives the Regions time to conclude the agreement; it does not validate the provision.

Confidence: *official*, operative part read in the text of the judgment.

## The prior opinions

The Court notes that the annulled provision **itself stated** that it applied only "as long as the cooperation agreement [...] has not entered into force". The text thus acknowledged that the agreement was needed.

It also refers to **Council of State opinion no. 71.119/4 of 23 March 2022**, delivered before the vote, itself based on **opinion no. 63.920/1/V of 26 October 2018**. Chronology: Council of State opinion in October 2018, new opinion in March 2022, adoption in June 2022, annulment in December 2024.

## Where the negotiations stand

On **8 May 2026**, MP Pascal Smet tabled a request for explanations entitled "taxis to and from Brussels Airport and the related cooperation agreement". The answer is dated **9 June 2026**.

It is addressed to the **State Secretary for Housing, Paid Passenger Transport and Sports Infrastructure**. Taxis do not fall under the Minister for Mobility: the competence is titled "paid passenger transport".

According to the record, the State Secretary states that she has not "yet had time to start the negotiations" with her two counterparts, having taken office three months earlier. The last contacts between the three regional administrations date, according to her, from before the judgment of December 2024.

She announces a bilateral meeting with the Flemish Region, with the aim of reaching conclusions "by the end of 2026" and making progress "in the first half of 2027". She also sets a limit: an agreement must first cover the airport, and if the two other Regions demand reciprocity, that is, the right for their taxis to pick up customers in Brussels, "we will refuse it".

Confidence: *official*, record of the sitting of 9 June 2026.

In July 2005, **Pascal Smet**, then a Brussels minister, proposed that a taxi that had dropped a passenger off at the airport should be able to pick one up for the return trip, according to a question put in the Flemish Parliament on 28 September 2005. In the debate of 9 June 2026, he states that, as minister, he came up against the refusal of the municipality of Zaventem and of the local taxi companies.

## The Zaventem case

Brussels Airport is located in Zaventem, **in the Flemish Region**. Since taxis are a regional competence, the link between Brussels and its airport crosses a regional border.

According to the State Secretary (9 June 2026), a Brussels taxi may freely **drop off** at the airport, but may only **pick up** a trip booked in advance; no Region authorises a taxi from another Region to park on its territory while waiting for a customer. In the same debate, Pascal Smet described an additional condition, that the booked trip must end outside Brussels. The record does not settle between the two readings.

The arrivals queue is governed by a **regulation of the municipality of Zaventem**: it is reserved for holders of a parking authorisation issued by the municipality, capped at **200** since 1 April 2024. The quota has been reached and the waiting list runs until 2029.

In Flanders, a street taxi, a category that covers platform services, may not be within **two hundred metres** of the airport grounds except for a booked trip (order of the Flemish Government of 9 June 2023, article 38).

The Flemish decree of 29 March 2019 nevertheless removed the link between the authorisation and the municipality, the authorisation now being valid throughout Flemish territory. Access to the airport **rank** has however remained municipal: this reform did not change access to the arrivals queue.

In 2010, the Brussels Region asked for its taxis to be allowed to pick up in Zaventem; the municipality refused, according to BRUZZ (19 October 2010).

## The appeals against the ordinance

In 2022, an operator with Flemish licences challenged the Brussels ordinance. The appeal that led to the annulment was lodged by **SA Taxis Autolux**, a taxi company active since 1934, with its registered office in Brussels. The judgment summarises its position: the largest part of its activity concerns trips between the capital and the airport, and it states that **ninety per cent of its licences are Flemish**. The conditions laid down in 2022 made interregional trips, in its view, impossible or extremely difficult.

In its judgment no. 40/2012 of 8 March 2012, the Court had upheld the territorial connection provided for by the 1995 ordinance, which links the trip to the place where it begins. The 2024 judgment concerns another provision, the 2022 one on Flemish and Walloon licences, and annuls it on another ground, the absence of a cooperation agreement.

Between 2005 and 2020, the fleet fluctuates between 1,224 and 1,298 vehicles, a range of six per cent. Over the same period, the number of **licensed drivers** falls from 3,556 in 2006 to 1,757 in 2020, a halving in fourteen years, with a constant fleet.

⚠️ **Do not link this series to today's figures.** Until 2022, taxis and hire cars with driver fall under two separate regimes, and the older series count only taxis. Since the 2022 ordinance, the former LVC (hire cars with driver) have become "street taxis" and are part of the same whole. The shift from around 1,250 to around 3,100 vehicles between 2020 and 2026 is first and foremost a **change of legal scope**, not growth in the fleet.

## The quota is not the fleet

The order of 6 October 2022 sets the numerus clausus at **1,425 rank taxis and 1,825 street taxis**, or 3,250. This is a **ceiling**.

The official register of operators published on **11 September 2026** prints a total of **3,143 vignettes**. The 3,091 legible lines of the document correspond to 2,095 operators; the largest holds twenty-three. The register does not distinguish rank taxis from street taxis. The latest published breakdown dates from 20 May 2025: **1,407 rank taxis and 1,758 street taxis** authorised.

The number of certificates far exceeds that of active drivers: **8,621 certificates of competence issued for 3,831 drivers declared active** (parliamentary answer of 2 July 2025).

## Taxis and platforms

### Who is what

Since the ordinance of 9 June 2022, in force on 21 October 2022, hire cars with driver (LVC), including the drivers of Uber, Bolt or Heetch, have become **street taxis**. A street taxi does not carry the visual identity of a taxi, cannot be hailed or wait at ranks, and works only on booking. The **rank taxi** keeps the taximeter, the ranks and hailing.

The platforms do not operate taxis. The ordinance makes them **booking intermediaries**, subject to regional approval. The list updated on 1 August 2026 contains more than eighty, among them Uber Belgium, Bolt Operations (an Estonian company), Heetch Belgium, Taxi Radio Bruxellois (Taxis Verts), Victor Cab and the Unit cooperative, which runs the Uride app. The Minister-President announced 131 of them on 23 January 2024. The gap between these two official figures is not explained.

### Rank taxis are on the apps too

On 5 October 2022, Uber launched the Uber Taxi option in Brussels, which allows rank taxis to receive trips through the app, the final price being set by the taximeter. On 23 January 2024, the Minister-President stated in committee: "We know that, at a minimum, Uber, Heetch and Bolt allocate their trips to street taxis and to rank taxis." A rank taxi that accepts a trip booked through this channel applies the fare for booked trips, and may refuse it.

On 18 September 2025, the increase in Bolt's commission was denounced as "a new hard blow for drivers" by the Fédération Belge des Taxis, which represents rank taxi operators.

The boundary between taxis and platforms is therefore legal, not economic: the same rank vehicle can take a customer hailed in the street and a trip ordered via an app. How many rank taxis work this way, and with which apps, is not published. The question of how many companies operate both rank taxis and street taxis was put to Parliament on 23 January 2024; the answer does not give the number.

### Market shares are not known

No public data makes it possible to divide the market between rank taxis, street taxis and platforms, whether in trips, turnover or drivers. The administration states that it has "no consolidated data on the number of trips carried out" (answer of 2 July 2025).

The only numerical estimate found comes from a platform. Before the Parliament's special committee on Uber, on 16 February 2023, the managing director of Heetch estimated that his company accounted for "25 or 30%" of the Brussels market before the reform and "15 to 20%" after, without defining that market. Confidence: *unconfirmed*, statement by an operator.

### Prices

The order of 6 October 2022 sets two regimes.

| | Rank taxi hailed or taken at a rank (art. 7) | Rank taxi on booking and street taxi (art. 8) |
|---|---|---|
| Pick-up charge | 2.60 EUR | at least 1.50 EUR |
| Price per kilometre | 2.30 EUR | at least 1.50 EUR |
| Price per minute | 0.60 EUR | at least 0.40 EUR |
| Minimum fare | 8 EUR | 8 EUR |
| Ceiling | fixed fare | 200% of the minimums |

A hailed rank taxi applies a fixed fare. A booked trip, by a street taxi or by a rank taxi, falls between a minimum and double that minimum: between 1.50 and 3.00 EUR per kilometre, for example. Comparing 1.50 EUR and 2.30 EUR per kilometre therefore means comparing a floor with a fixed fare.

In 2024, these amounts were not indexed: the order rounds indexed amounts down to the nearest multiple of 50 cents, and the indexation did not reach that threshold (parliamentary answer of 17 April 2024).

### Commissions

The commission taken by the platform on the price of the trip is at the heart of street taxi drivers' grievances.

- **Uber**: "25%" on average in Brussels, with variable pricing "ranging from 3 to 35%" depending on the trip, according to the company on 26 November 2024; "it is therefore incorrect to say that Uber has increased its commission".
- **Bolt**: from 19.8% to 23.8% on 1 October 2025, in Brussels, in the Flemish periphery and at the airport, according to RTBF.
- **Unit (Uride)**: the cooperative launched in March 2026 claims some 90 members, including 80 drivers, and presents its app as an alternative to the platforms, according to L'Avenir (30 March 2026).

At the Regional Advisory Committee of 19 May 2026, the administration noted that "some intermediaries take a commission of up to 35%". The regulation of commissions falls under both the federal level and the Region; a full mapping is announced for 2027.

### The grievances, according to who voices them

The positions are those of the actors cited, not findings.

- **Fédération Belge des Taxis (FeBeT)**, rank taxi operators. In October 2022, its president stated that "Uber has been working illegally for eight years with 'limousine' licences while offering taxi services" (La Libre, 11 October 2022). In May 2026, the federation calls for "an end to unfair competition" and for "the minimum price per kilometre to be the same for everyone" (La Libre, 20 May 2026).
- **Street taxi drivers** working with the apps. On the eve of their action on 2 April 2026, they denounce frozen fares and rising commissions, and call for "a guaranteed minimum net price, excluding commission" as well as access to the dedicated lanes reserved for rank taxis (L'Avenir, 1 April 2026).
- **Uber** disputes having increased its commission (26 November 2024, see above).

On **18 September 2026**, the demonstration denouncing "ten years of asphyxiation" in the face of "competition from multinationals such as Uber" had as its spokesperson Jean-Paul Mbenti, of the Association belge des chauffeurs limousine (ABCL), which represents street taxi drivers, according to Belga, as reported by the RTBF. He is also the founder of the Unit cooperative. The demonstrators were therefore, according to the sources, street taxi drivers, the very ones who work with the platforms.

### The status of drivers

The social status of drivers is a federal matter: the act of 3 October 2022 created a presumption of employment for platform work, and European directive 2024/2831 must be transposed by 2 December 2026. No decision applying this presumption to a Brussels taxi driver has been found.

## The transfer of authorisations

The regime for transferring authorisations has been amended four times:

- **2002**: transfer becomes possible after ten years of holding;
- **2006**: it is **abolished**;
- **2013**: it is **reinstated**, under the supervision of a price assessment commission;
- **2022**: the ordinance buys back transferred licences at **35,000 EUR**.

In November 2020, a parliamentary answer put the transfer value of a taxi authorisation between **zero and 120,000 EUR**, and that of a limousine between zero and 40,000 EUR.

## Inspection

In **2010**, the Region had **five inspectors, two of them operational**, for 552 official reports and **three** vehicle seizures over the year (parliamentary answer of 15 April 2011).

In March 2023, the team numbered **seven judicial police officers** for the whole Region. From **November 2022 to December 2023**, Brussels Mobility carried out **4,540 inspections**: 3,281 with no further action, 604 with at least one warning and **655 official reports** (Brussels Parliament committee, 23 January 2024). The most frequent offences concerned the absence of a certificate of competence, an operating authorisation or an employment contract, the absence of a vignette or fare information, and the use of bus lanes by street taxis.

## Actors with diverging interests

The 2024 judgment joins **four appeals** against the same text, brought by parties with opposing interests: the Union des Chauffeurs Limousine Belge and some thirty companies, SA Taxis Autolux, the Fédération Belge des Taxis, and SA Taxi Radio Bruxellois, better known as Taxis Verts.

The composition of the **Regional Advisory Committee**, communicated to Parliament on 12 March 2024, is as follows: seven operators' associations, **three** drivers' unions, four platforms, the national grouping, the social fund and the association of Brussels hotels. The operators' associations have more than twice as many seats as the drivers' unions. The four seats reserved for platforms were allocated by **drawing lots** among eight candidates.

According to a parliamentary answer of 12 May 2020, the committee did **not meet in 2019 or in 2020**; the mandates designated in 2013 remained in force until the new designation order of March 2024.

On 14 January 2020, drivers of hire cars with driver demonstrated on Place Poelaert in support of eight Uber drivers on trial against the Fédération Belge des Taxis, according to a parliamentary question. On 14 November 2024, according to BRUZZ, a demonstration of drivers that set off from Uber's offices stopped at the headquarters of a union that the demonstrators accused of supporting the platform.

## The missing data

Three findings drawn from ministerial answers:

- **no consolidated data on the number of trips** carried out is available;
- neither the Flemish Region nor the Brussels Region has figures on inspections, fines or empty trips at the airport;
- the **evaluation of the 2022 ordinance**, provided for by the text and due in 2025, had not started as of 13 July 2026; the State Secretary says she has entrusted it to Brussels Mobility, without a timetable.

## Zero-emission deadline: two dates

The deadline requiring taxis to acquire zero-emission vehicles was postponed to 1 January 2027 by the **ordinance of 12 December 2024**. But article 4, § 1 of the order of 6 October 2022 still bears the date of **1 January 2025**. The ordinance prevails; the order has not been aligned.

On 1 September 2026, according to BRUZZ, a majority party called for a further postponement to 2029. See the [Mobility page](/en/domains/mobility) for the follow-up of this aspect, and the [Shared mobility dossier](/en/dossiers/mobilite-partagee) for the framework of shared services.
