## What the urban revitalisation zone is

<Essentiel>

The urban revitalisation zone (ZRU, from the French *zone de revitalisation urbaine*) is a boundary that the Brussels government sets by statistical sector. The organic ordinance of 6 October 2016 sets three cumulative conditions: a median income below the regional median income, a population density above the regional average and an unemployment rate above the regional average. These criteria are relative: each sector is compared with the most recent regional values. The ordinance allows sectors to be added or removed. Sustainable neighbourhood contracts and urban renovation contracts are carried out in this zone.

</Essentiel>

<Complet>

**Legal basis.** Article 4 of the ordinance of 6 October 2016 defines the ZRU as a zone, in principle continuous, made up of statistical sectors, in which the combination of the cumulative conditions of Article 5, § 2, shows that residents' living conditions are worse than in the Region's other statistical sectors, so that the standard of living there is lower. Article 5, § 2, sets the three conditions. The French-language perspective.brussels page on the ZRU speaks of a median income below the regional « moyenne » (average); the ordinance says regional median income. The government must update the zone whenever it deems it necessary and in any case within twelve months of the new government taking office (Article 6, § 1).

**What it is for.** Urban revitalisation programmes are carried out in the ZRU (Article 7), with the exception of part of the City Policy. The government selects within it the areas of sustainable neighbourhood contracts (Article 20) and the study areas of urban renovation contracts (Article 40); properties directly adjoining these areas can also be funded. For strand 2 of the City Policy, devoted to neighbourhood development, grants may only be used in the ZRU (Article 60), and each municipality's budget partly reflects the number of its residents who live in the zone (Article 62).

**Three generations of boundary.** The ZRU 2016 was adopted by decree of 10 November 2016. The ZRU 2020 was adopted by decree of 14 November 2019 and applied from January 2020 to August 2026. The ZRU 2026 was adopted by decree of 23 July 2026, published in the Belgian Official Gazette on 26 August 2026 and in force since 1 September 2026.

**Before the ZRU.** The regional evaluation of April 2018 traces the boundaries that preceded it: the zone to be protected and renovated defined by the King Baudouin Foundation in 1988, the area for the reinforced development of housing (EDRL) in the 1995 Regional Development Plan, then the area for the reinforced development of housing and renovation (EDRLR) in the 2002 plan. On 16 July 2013, in a committee of the Brussels Parliament, the Minister-President presented the future ZRU as a radical departure from the EDRLR criteria, which in his words were based on the quality and age of the buildings. Neighbourhood contracts themselves date from a 1993 ordinance; they became sustainable neighbourhood contracts in 2010.

**The « croissant pauvre ».** The expression (literally, the poor crescent) is older than the ZRU. The report of the United Assembly of the Common Community Commission on the state of poverty of 2 July 1998 already refers to certain neighbourhoods that some authors call the « croissant pauvre de Bruxelles ». On 15 December 2016, in his answer to written question no. 280 from Alain Destexhe, the Minister-President wrote that neighbourhood contracts target the EDRLR, which he described as commonly called the « croissant pauvre ». It is an informal name; the boundary that has legal effects is the ZRU.

</Complet>

## What the 2026 boundary changes

<Essentiel>

The decree of 23 July 2026 sets the ZRU 2026, in force since 1 September 2026. According to perspective.brussels, 177 statistical sectors meet the three conditions, in 18 of the 19 municipalities, compared with 171 in 2020. Of these 177, 155 already met them in 2020, or 88%, and 151 since at least 2016.

The adopted boundary comprises 208 statistical sectors. According to perspective.brussels, its 206 complete sectors cover 27.7 km², or 17.1% of the regional territory, and have 505,703 residents, or 40.4% of the population. On the BruGIS layers used for the map, the area goes from 30.68 km² (official value of the 2020 layer) to 27.82 km² (BGM calculation on the 2026 layer). Tour & Taxis and Biestebroeck, included in 2020 as specific areas (development sites, not neighbourhoods meeting the three conditions), leave the zone; Forest prison joins it.

</Essentiel>

<Complet>

**The 2026 thresholds.** The perspective.brussels report of June 2026 uses a regional median taxable income of 24,488 euros (2023 incomes), below which 259 sectors lie, or 36%; an average regional density of 7,731.67 inhabitants per km² (2025), exceeded by 461 sectors, or 64%; and an average regional unemployment rate of 17.28% (2023), exceeded by 212 sectors, or 29%. The 177 sectors meeting all three conditions represent 24% of the Region's sectors. Woluwe-Saint-Pierre is the only municipality with none; in Saint-Josse-ten-Noode, 71.4% of sectors meet them, in Molenbeek-Saint-Jean 60.9% and in Saint-Gilles 57.7%.

**From 177 to 208 sectors.** The decree lists six composition principles. Around a main continuous zone of 138 sectors, it adds isolated sectors, keeps sectors distant from the continuous zone, adds sectors that meet two conditions and whose third deviates by less than 5% from the regional threshold, and adds five specific areas: the Gare du Midi area, the Gare du Nord area, the Gare de l'Ouest area, the Abattoir-Cureghem neighbourhoods and Forest prison. The perspective.brussels report calls the second one « Quartier Nord ». The decree specifies that « sortants » (outgoing) sectors and « de liaison » (linking) sectors are not to be added to the zone.

**Two ways of counting areas.** The official perspective.brussels figures cover complete sectors: the 27.7 km² for 2026 exclude the two partially included sectors, which have 4,070 residents. For 2020, the 2019 note gives 30.91 km² without saying whether this area excludes its 8 partial sectors; its population figure does exclude them (4,209 residents). The layers published on BruGIS cover the whole boundary, partial sectors included: the 2020 layer carries an official area of 30.68 km², and BGM measures 27.82 km² on the 2026 layer. Only these two values can be compared directly. The population shares cannot be compared directly either: 43.36% in 2020 and 40.4% in 2026 are calculated on regional populations from different years.

**What the map shows.** It overlays the 2020 and 2026 outlines published on BruGIS. The 2020 area (30.68 km²) is the official attribute of the layer; the 2026 area (27.82 km²) and the list of sectors joining or leaving are BGM calculations, made by matching the statistical sectors against the two outlines: according to this calculation, 25 sectors join and 31 leave. These counts are not official figures. The perspective.brussels report uses « entrants » and « sortants » (incoming and outgoing) in another sense, that of Article 5, § 3, of the ordinance, and counts 13 and 55 of them.

**The link in the decree.** Article 2 of the decree refers to the report through a quartiers.brussels link that returned a 404 error on 24 September 2026. The report is available on the perspective.brussels website.

</Complet>

## What it changes for you

<Essentiel>

Effects confirmed by the texts in force:

- **Sustainable neighbourhood contracts and urban renovation contracts.** They are carried out in the ZRU; properties directly adjoining their areas can also benefit.
- **School Contract.** It is open to schools in the zone that serve a vulnerable pupil population, but the ordinance of 16 May 2019 still refers to the ZRU 2016.
- **ERDF 2021-2027.** The European programme gives priority support to these contracts, located in the ZRU.
- **Ongoing programmes.** A programme approved under the ZRU 2020 remains governed by it until its final account, even if its sector leaves the zone.

The renovation grants currently in force (RENOLUTION, decree of 31 March 2022) contain no criterion linked to the ZRU, and the increased allowance on registration duties, which applied to the former EDRLR, was repealed by the ordinance of 12 December 2016.

</Essentiel>

<Complet>

**Sustainable neighbourhood contracts.** Article 20 of the ordinance of 6 October 2016 provides that the government determines, within the urban revitalisation zone, the areas eligible for subsidies. According to quartiers.brussels, the Region contributes 14.125 million euros per programme, and the municipality at least 5% of the programme amount. For urban renovation contracts, which cover several municipalities, Article 40 provides that the study areas are chosen within the urban revitalisation zone; perspective.brussels states 22 million euros of investment per contract for contracts 1 to 7.

**School Contract.** Article 4, § 2, of the ordinance of 16 May 2019 requires the project to concern a school located in the urban revitalisation zone (ZRU) that serves a vulnerable pupil population. Article 2, 18°, defines this zone as the one in the decree of 10 November 2016, known as the ZRU 2016, and Justel shows no amendment to this definition. Read literally, neither the ZRU 2020 nor the ZRU 2026 therefore applies to the School Contract; the perspective.brussels page on the School Contract simply refers to the ZRU. A contract can reach at most 2.5 million euros, over four years.

**ERDF 2021-2027.** The programme states that the ERDF will strengthen regional programmes (sustainable neighbourhood contracts and urban renovation contracts) and that its intervention will benefit residents of neighbourhoods in the urban revitalisation zone (p. 8). For these contracts, it specifies that their areas must imperatively lie within the urban revitalisation zone (ZRU) defined by the ordinance (p. 68). Elsewhere, for green spaces, it refers to the urban revitalisation zone as defined by the PRDD, the Regional Sustainable Development Plan (p. 59).

**Programmes approved under the ZRU 2020.** The decree of 23 July 2026 provides that programmes and operations approved on the basis of the ZRU 2020 and in the execution, implementation or justification phase remain governed by the ZRU 2020 until notification of the final account.

**Grants and registration duties.** The perspective.brussels report of June 2026 still lists, among the instruments implemented in the ZRU, grants for renovation and facade embellishment and registration duties. These grants were repealed by a decree of 23 December 2021. The decree of 31 March 2022, which organises the RENOLUTION grants, mentions neither the ZRU, nor revitalisation, nor the EDRLR; Bruxelles Environnement stated on 24 September 2026 that these grants were suspended. For registration duties, the former Article 46bis of the Code raised the allowance to 75,000 euros when the property was located in an area for the reinforced development of housing and renovation; Article 14 of the ordinance of 12 December 2016 repealed this paragraph. The general allowance falls under a commitment tracked by BGM, with no link to the ZRU.

**A framework announced as set to change.** The regional policy declaration of 13 February 2026 announces that the government will merge the various urban renovation plans (sustainable neighbourhood contracts, City Policy, School Contract, « contrat d'Ilot », urban renovation contracts) into two main instruments, the sustainable neighbourhood contract and the urban renovation contract, and that railway stations will be the priority zones of this approach (p. 14).

## Since 1994: what was promised, done and evaluated

<Essentiel>

Since 1994, 98 neighbourhood contracts and sustainable neighbourhood contracts have been launched in 14 municipalities (BGM count). The 92 programmes with a published amount, launched from 1994 to 2021, total 1.198 billion euros (BGM sum): budgets allocated in current euros, not expenditure, and a minimum, because the 2022 and 2023 series have no published amount.

The last overall evaluation published, carried out by BDO (April 2018), covers the contracts launched from 2007 to 2017. It judges the interventions relevant, but without sufficient scale to structurally influence regional indicators, and counts 790 housing units produced. On residential moves, it finds that each municipality's property market dynamics weigh more than the neighbourhood contract, which in its words does not have the power to correct market effects and population movements.

The Court of Audit reviewed the first six contracts in 2000 (final report in February 2001); since then, it has published no audit devoted to them (BGM check).

</Essentiel>

<Complet>

<ZruProgrammes locale="en" />

**The count.** The Programmes page of quartiers.brussels lists 13 series of neighbourhood contracts (56 programmes, 1994-2013) and 13 series of sustainable neighbourhood contracts (42 programmes, 2010-2028). Series 12 and 13 of the sustainable neighbourhood contracts, launched in 2022 and 2023, have no published amount; they are the ones that add Berchem-Sainte-Agathe, Evere and Ganshoren to the list. The 92 programmes with a published amount are therefore in 11 municipalities. Adding up the published amounts gives 620,286,950 euros for neighbourhood contracts and 578,188,042 euros for sustainable neighbourhood contracts, or 1,198,474,992 euros. The Dutch version of the same page gives, for series 1 and 2 of the sustainable neighbourhood contracts, the amount of series 4, for a total of 1,193,264,830 euros. The page does not say which funders its amounts cover: they are not limited to the Region's share.

**The 2018 evaluation.** Carried out by BDO for Brussels Urbanism and Heritage, it covers 34 contracts (12 neighbourhood contracts and 22 sustainable neighbourhood contracts) undertaken between January 2007 and January 2017. It finds a very positive rate of commitment and completion, with the relative exception of the housing strand, for which, it says, the initial ambitions systematically exceed what was delivered. Of the 790 housing units produced, 613 are treated as social housing. It puts the total of the interventions over ten years at about 501 million euros, including the Beliris envelope (note 65), and describes this amount as a budget that is both substantial and modest (p. 69). It also writes that the 34 contracts evaluated represent a public investment (municipal, regional, federal) of more than 770 million euros (p. 33). The sentence on market effects and population movements appears in its analysis of the neighbourhoods (p. 138): according to the evaluation, the neighbourhood contract mitigates them locally. The Documentation page of quartiers.brussels lists two evaluations: this one, and a 2018 thematic study on gender and the sports facilities of sustainable neighbourhood contracts.

**The Court of Audit.** Its 12th Cahier d'observations addressed to the Brussels Parliament (158th Cahier, 2001-2002 session) contains an article on the implementation of the first neighbourhood contracts (pp. 14 to 59): a check of the legality and regularity of the expenditure of the first six contracts, launched in 1994, whose final report was sent on 28 February 2001. BGM went through the 117 publications in the Brussels-Capital Region section of the Court's website and the full text of its Brussels Cahiers 9 to 30: after 2001, neighbourhood contracts appear there only in budget comments (commitments, outstanding amounts), with no audit devoted to them. In 2007, the Court examined for the Chamber the federal major cities policy (city and housing contracts 2005-2007), which funded seven Brussels municipalities; this is a federal scheme distinct from the regional neighbourhood contracts.

**What the regional policy declaration says.** The declaration of 13 February 2026 promises a culture of public policy evaluation (p. 9) and states that the government will ensure that the recommendations issued by the Court of Audit are implemented in order to strengthen financial and budgetary transparency (p. 4): this commitment concerns public finances, not programme evaluation. It names the ZRU once, for building insulation and green energy production, in particular in the neighbourhoods of the zone (p. 19).

**Open question.** Is an evaluation of the contracts launched since 2017, and of the urban renovation contracts, planned, and when will it be published?

## And in Europe?

<Essentiel>

In 2025, according to Eurostat, 23.3% of residents of the Brussels-Capital Region have an income below the at-risk-of-poverty threshold, set at 60% of the national median income, compared with 10.9% for Belgium as a whole: about twice the national rate (BGM calculation). Among the ten capital regions in the chart, this is the highest ratio. Vienna has a higher rate, 25.7%, against an Austrian rate of 15.9%.

<ZruPointsEurope locale="en" />

</Essentiel>

<Complet>

**Why a ratio rather than a ranking.** Each rate is calculated relative to the median income of its own country: thresholds differ from one country to another, and the same rate does not describe the same standard of living. The chart therefore compares each region with its own country, without ranking standards of living across countries. The rate comes from the EU-SILC survey: it is an estimate, and differences of a few points between two regions may remain within the margin of uncertainty. The [poverty in capital regions](/en/comparisons/poverty-capital-regions) card details the comparison, and the explainer on the [Brussels paradox](/en/explainers/brussels-paradox) shows why a region with high output can have residents on modest incomes.

**Why we do not compare with London or New York.** Thresholds, surveys and boundaries are not the same, and a ranking would suggest a precision the data do not have. One example is enough to show it: in London, the share of residents in poverty goes from 15% to 26% depending on whether housing costs are counted (Trust for London, three-year averages up to 2023/24, threshold of 60% of median income).

</Complet>

## Who is responsible for what

<Essentiel>

The Region sets the ZRU and funds urban renewal. Municipalities contribute at least 5% of the amount of each sustainable neighbourhood contract. The federal State funds Beliris and sets the right to social integration, paid out by the 19 CPASs. The Common Community Commission is responsible for the report on the state of poverty.

None of the 16 commitments from the regional policy declaration tracked by BGM concerns the ZRU or urban renewal.

</Essentiel>

<Complet>

**The commitments tracked.** BGM tracks 16 commitments from the regional policy declaration of 13 February 2026; none concerns the ZRU, sustainable neighbourhood contracts, urban renovation contracts, the School Contract, the City Policy or poverty. Two related commitments should not be confused with the ZRU: the allowance on registration duties, which concerns the general ceiling, and railway station safety, since the declaration also designates stations as priority zones for revitalisation (p. 14).

## Method and limitations

<Essentiel>

Tax income leaves out the social integration income, family allowances and the income of most people working for international organisations. The European at-risk-of-poverty rate comes from a sample survey and is calculated on a national threshold. The series have breaks: in 2019 for the SILC survey, in 2020 for the administrative rate. Areas, counts and ratios marked "BGM calculation" may differ from official figures.

</Essentiel>

<Complet>

**Tax income and standard of living.** The Monitoring des Quartiers specifies that total net taxable income, the basis of indicator 2498, does not include the social integration income granted by CPASs or family allowances, generally underestimates income from movable and immovable property, and does not cover untaxed income, such as that of most people working for international organisations. People living in collective households and households with zero taxable income are excluded.

**SILC survey.** The at-risk-of-poverty rate of the Brussels-Capital Region published by Statbel is 31.4% in 2019, 28.2% in 2020, 25.4% in 2021, 28.5% in 2022, 28.8% in 2023, 26.3% in 2024 and 23.3% in 2025. Eurostat reports a break in series in 2019, and Statbel marks 2020 with an asterisk for the impact of the COVID-19 crisis on the survey. Statbel and the 2025 Social Barometer warn that year-to-year differences are uncertain; according to the Barometer, interpreting the fluctuations of these rates from one year to the next would be uncertain: this series does not support conclusions about a trend.

**Administrative rate.** It is calculated on exhaustive administrative data, but Statbel reports a break in 2020 (improved methodology) and municipalities where a significant share of residents is not taken into account.

**Geometry.** Map outlines are simplified (25-metre tolerance) from the layers published on BruGIS (CC BY 4.0 licence for the service, CC0 for the ZRU layers). Each figure states its producer, its source, its extraction date and the changes made.

**Data date.** The figures in this dossier were collected on 24 September 2026.
