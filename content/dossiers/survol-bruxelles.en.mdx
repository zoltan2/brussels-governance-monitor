## Five pilots dazzled by lasers on approach, no perpetrator identified (14-15 August 2026)

On the night of **Friday 14 to Saturday 15 August 2026**, five pilots on approach to Brussels Airport reported being dazzled by **laser beams** over **Dilbeek**. The first report came at **21:45**, the last at **02:43**. A local police patrol was dispatched without finding those responsible, as the pilots could not pinpoint the source. Five incidents in a single night is an unusually high total.

On social media, opponents of the flight path were quickly blamed. A **fact-check published on 20 August** by a Flemish magazine concludes that nothing establishes this, and that a nearby electronic music festival is an equally plausible hypothesis. Neither can be demonstrated as things stand.

This file therefore retains only what is established: five reports, one night, no identified perpetrator, no arrest. The episode is recorded here because it illustrates a shift in the conflict, from the legal and institutional arena towards public blame, and because the accusation travelled faster than its verification.

Sources: [VRT NWS (17 August 2026)](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/en/2026/08/17/five-pilots-landing-at-brussels-airport-blinded-by-lasers/) ; [Knack, fact-check (20 August 2026)](https://www.knack.be/factcheck/factcheck-waren-de-lasers-van-een-brussels-muziekfestival-zichtbaar-tot-in-de-cockpit-van-een-landend-vliegtuig/). Confidence: official for the five reports, their timings and the absence of an identified perpetrator; unconfirmed for any attribution, explicitly ruled out by the fact-check.

## Forty-two families from the periphery sue the Belgian state over the RNP 07L route (11 August 2026)

On **Tuesday 11 August 2026**, the citizens' collective **Bruegelvogelvrij** announced that **42 families** from **Dilbeek**, **Lennik** and **Roosdaal**, in Flemish Brabant, are suing the **Belgian state** over noise from the **RNP 07L** approach route, nicknamed the "Crucke route" in the Dutch-language press after the federal mobility minister.

This satellite-guided route begins the descent to Brussels Airport's runway 07L far west of the airport, over Lennik, then passes over the north-west of the Brussels Region, notably **Molenbeek-Saint-Jean**, **Koekelberg** and **Berchem-Sainte-Agathe**. On busy days an aircraft begins its approach **every two to three minutes**, including at night.

### The return of the easterly wind reopened the file

Introduced as a **temporary trial in 2025**, the route was **extended to October 2026**. The easterly wind's return in early August 2026 brought the noise back: under that wind regime, Brussels Airport favours approaches from the west.

### Three proceedings, three institutional positions

Bruegelvogelvrij's action is **separate** from the cease-and-desist action brought by the **Brussels Region** in June 2026, and from the step the municipality of **Lennik** announced it was preparing. Three sets of proceedings therefore target the same route from three different positions: Flemish residents, a region, a Flemish municipality. It is a convergence of aims, not a joint action.

**What this does not establish**: the figure of 42 families comes from the collective, no court has ruled, and no hearing date has been announced at this stage.

## The conflict in brief

Brussels Airport (Zaventem), located 12 km from the centre of Brussels, has been at the heart of a conflict spanning more than **40 years** between the airport's economic interests (24,000 direct jobs, 64,225 total jobs, 5.4 Bln EUR in added value) and the quality of life of **670,600 Brussels residents** exposed to aircraft noise. The Superior Health Council estimates the health cost at **more than 1 billion EUR per year**.

The structural problem: prevailing winds (south-west, 70% of the time) require take-offs on runway 25R **towards Brussels**, overflying densely populated neighbourhoods. When the wind blows from the east, landings on runway 07L bring aircraft over the north and north-west of the city. And when the wind blows from the north, runway 01 brings aircraft over the east of Brussels.

In 2025, the airport handled **24.4 million passengers** (+3.3% vs 2024) and **795,000 tonnes of cargo** (+8.5%). Belgium has paid **~25 million EUR in cumulative penalties** for aircraft nuisance.

## The three runways and their noise corridors

Brussels Airport has three runways:

| Runway | Length | Orientation | Noise corridor |
|--------|--------|-------------|----------------|
| **25R/07L** | 3,638 m | East-West | Take-offs 25R: central/northern BXL (canal). Landings 07L: Jette, Koekelberg, Molenbeek-Saint-Jean, Anderlecht (north), City of Brussels (Northern Quarter, Tour & Taxis), Laeken (outside the 1,500 m Royal Castle perimeter), Schaerbeek |
| **25L/07R** | 3,211 m | East-West | Parallel (to the south of) the 25R/07L. Take-offs 25L westward and landings 07R on a parallel track further south: corridor includes Anderlecht and City of Brussels (Pentagon, excluding Laeken) |
| **01/19** | 2,987 m | South-North | Landings 01: eastern BXL (Woluwe-Saint-Pierre, Kraainem, Wezembeek-Oppem) |

### Canal Route (runway 25R)
Departures following the axis of the Brussels canal, impacting the centre and north of the Region. This is historically the most-used route (54% of all movements).

### Crucke Route / RNP-07L
The most controversial route. Activated in summer 2025 as a "temporary alternative procedure" during airport works, it uses satellite navigation (RNP — Required Navigation Performance) for straight-in approaches on runway 07L. Unlike previous curved approaches, it **concentrates 100% of flights on the exact same corridor**, amplifying the nuisance.

Key data:
- **96% of RNP-07L flights** violate Brussels noise standards
- **64 violation reports** issued in a single month (January 2026)
- **450,000 Brussels residents** affected (estimate by the mayors' joint front)
- Route extended until **31 October 2026** (repeated extensions since summer 2025)

The federal minister describes the RNP-07L as a "purely alternative procedure" rather than a "preferential route". Critics note it is activated even in moderate easterly winds (4-6 knots).

### Runway 01: the eastern corridor

Runway 01/19 is the diagonal runway (south-north). Landings on runway 01 overfly **Woluwe-Saint-Pierre, Kraainem, Sterrebeek and Wezembeek-Oppem** at low altitude. Until 2004, runway 01 was only used exceptionally for landings.

| Period | Daytime use (runway 01) | Night-time use (runway 01) |
|--------|------------------------|---------------------------|
| 1997-2003 | 6.7% | 2.9% |
| 2004-2021 | 13.2% (**+97%**) | 10.2% (**+252%**) |

The sharp increase is linked to the modification of wind thresholds (see section below). In December 2023, **1,400 residents** of the eastern corridor won their case in court: **6M EUR** in damages + **20,000 EUR/day** in penalty payments as long as the situation persists.

### Royal Castle of Laeken exclusion zone
Since **1954**, a **1,500 m perimeter around the Royal Castle** prohibits overflights. This prohibition protects the **Royal Domain** itself — it does **not** shield the entire Laeken neighbourhood from the 07L approach corridor: its northern and western parts (Heysel, Mutsaard, surroundings of the basilica, King Baudouin Park) remain in the flight path. The prohibition only forces the diversion around the royal perimeter, shifting part of the nuisance onto neighbouring municipalities. In 2024, the N-VA proposed lifting this exclusion during the Arizona negotiations; MR and Les Engagés refused.

## Wind thresholds and Preferential Runway System (PRS)

The federal government imposes a **Preferential Runway System** (PRS) to distribute noise. The preferential runways are 25R, 25L and 19 (facing the prevailing south-westerly winds). Runways 01, 07L and 07R are **non-preferential** — they should only be used exceptionally.

The switching criterion is the **tailwind threshold** on runway 25: beyond the threshold, sKeyes activates a non-preferential runway. This threshold is at the heart of the controversy.

### Chronology of threshold modifications

| Period | Tailwind threshold (runway 25) | Effect |
|--------|-------------------------------|--------|
| Before 2004 | 8 knots average (WITHOUT gusts) | Runway 01 almost never used |
| 2004 (Anciaux plans) | Lowered to 5-7 knots | Sharp increase in runway 01 use |
| **17 Jul. 2013 (Wathelet instruction)** | **MAXWIND 12 knots (7 kt avg + 5 kt gust tolerance)** | **Only instruction ruled legal (Court of Appeal, 22/10/2020)** |
| Dec. 2013 (Durinckx instruction) | Unauthorised modification | **Declared illegal by the Court of Appeal** |
| March 2014 (DGTA) | 7 knots WITH gusts (5 kt tolerance removed) | **Runway 01 use doubled by day, tripled at night** |

**Key legal point**: the Brussels Court of Appeal ruled on 22 October 2020 that the Wathelet instruction of 17 July 2013 is **"the only legal, lawful instruction that does not violate any aviation regulation"**. All subsequent instructions were declared illegal. The State did **not** appeal to the Court of Cassation (March 2021). Yet in practice, sKeyes continues to apply the parameters from the illegal instructions.

**Anticipation**: sKeyes can activate a non-preferential runway **up to 2 hours before** wind conditions actually deteriorate, based on weather forecasts. This anticipation mechanism amplifies the use of runways 01 and 07L.

## Health impact

### Noise — Superior Health Council (May 2024)
The SHC published a scientific opinion on the health effects of air traffic. The health cost is estimated at **more than 1 billion EUR per year** around Brussels Airport:
- **160,000+ residents** at increased health risk in Brussels
- Aircraft noise is **more disruptive to sleep** than road or rail noise at equivalent decibel levels
- Documented effects: sleep disorders, cardiovascular diseases (hypertension, myocardial infarction), stress and depression, reduced lung function, learning difficulties in children, pregnancy complications, increased risk of type 2 diabetes
- **Recommendation**: ban on flights between 11 PM and 7 AM

### Ultrafine particles (air quality)

Beyond noise, air traffic emits **ultrafine particles (UFP)** that pose a distinct health risk.

**Brussels Airport study** (Atmospheric Environment, 2025): four measurement sites around the airport recorded concentrations of **6,500-14,000 particles/cm3** linked to aircraft, with peaks of **65,000-80,000 particles/cm3** during periods of high activity. Particles of 10-20 nm account for up to **65%** of the total number. Taxiing and take-off contribute **50-64%** of concentrations. The impact persists up to **7 km** from the airport.

**At the European level** (Transport & Environment, 2024): UFP from aircraft are associated with **280,000 cases of hypertension**, **330,000 cases of diabetes** and **18,000 cases of dementia**. **52 million Europeans** — more than 10% of the population — live in particularly exposed areas. The particles, smaller than 100 nanometres, penetrate deep into the body: they are found in the blood, brain and placental tissue.

The SHC deliberately broadened its 2024 analysis beyond noise to include this dimension.

### Recent international studies
- **JACC (February 2025)**: people exposed to night-time aircraft noise show **7% greater left ventricular mass** and **4% thicker cardiac walls**, indicators of increased cardiac risk.
- **Cardiovascular meta-analysis** (2024): cardiovascular risk increases by **+1.6% per 10 dB** of aircraft noise. Atrial fibrillation: **+2.7% per 10 dB**. Heart failure: **+2.3% per 10 dB**.
- **EEA (2025)**: transport noise in Europe causes **66,000 premature deaths/year**, **50,000 new cardiovascular cases/year** and **22,000 new type 2 diabetes cases/year**. Noise is the **3rd environmental health threat** in Europe, after air pollution and heat effects.

## Alternatives and potential solutions

### Freight transfer to Liege
Liege Airport handled **1.324 million tonnes of cargo** in 2025 (+14%), operates **24/7** and is already Belgium's largest cargo airport. During disruptions at Brussels, operators already redirect to Liege. **DHL transferred its European hub to Leipzig in 2008** after the refusal to extend night flights at Brussels — a departure that cost up to **1,700 jobs**.

### Continuous Descent Operations (CDO)
**Continuous Descent Operations** reduce noise by **1 to 5 dB per flight** on the ground by keeping the aircraft higher for longer. sKeyes tested them in 2022 (runways 25R/25L, 11 PM-6 AM) with Brussels Airlines, TUI, Ryanair and DHL. European potential: 150,000 tonnes of kerosene saved/year.

### Fleet modernisation
The Airbus A320neo is **~6 dB quieter** than the A320ceo — equivalent to: 4 neo take-offs = the noise of 1 ceo take-off. Brussels Airport aims for **63% next-generation aircraft by 2032**. The A350 and 787 operate several dB below the ICAO Chapter 14 minimums.

### New ICAO standards (CAEP/13, February 2025)
Future aircraft must be **at least 6 dB quieter** (-30% noise). Effective date: **1 January 2029**. A new **Chapter 15** requires supersonic aircraft to meet standards equivalent to subsonic aircraft.

## Balanced Approach procedure (EU)

On 27 January 2026, the **Balanced Approach procedure** (EU Regulation 598/2014) was launched, jointly led by the federal and Flemish governments. This mandatory procedure includes:
- A public consultation
- An opinion from the European Commission
- Assessment of 4 measures: noise reduction at source, land-use planning, operational procedures, operating restrictions

**Quantified target**: reduction of **30% of highly annoyed people** and **30% of people with severely disturbed sleep** by **2032** (compared to 2019).

It was triggered by the annulment of the environmental permit in July 2025. Flanders must re-decide by **June 2029**.

## Evere joins the Region's cessation action against RNP 07L (14 August 2026)

On **Friday 14 August 2026**, the municipality of **Evere** announced it was joining the **cessation action** brought by the Brussels-Capital Region against the intensive use of the **RNP 07L** flight path. The college of mayor and aldermen **appointed a lawyer** and the municipality formally joined the regional procedure.

### Two routes of challenge that should not be confused

The file contains legal actions of different kinds, which must be distinguished to avoid a misleading count of municipalities "in court":

- **Standalone municipal actions.** Koekelberg passed a motion and appointed a lawyer as early as **March 2026**, before the regional decision. Other north-western municipalities launched their own steps, documented earlier in this dossier.
- **Joining the regional action.** The Brussels government decided on **11 June 2026** to bring a cessation action against the federal State, and invited municipalities to join. According to RTBF and La Libre, **Schaerbeek** and **Berchem-Sainte-Agathe** had already done so; Evere is the **third** to follow.

Evere is therefore not the third Brussels municipality to go to court over this route, but the third to join the procedure brought by the Region.

### The arguments put forward

The municipality denounces an increase in low-altitude aircraft passages, repeated noise and night flights. The mayor stresses that what was meant to be a temporary measure cannot become a permanent situation at the expense of residents' peace, sleep and quality of life. RNP 07L entered service in **July 2025** as part of a temporary reorganisation of air traffic during works, and continued to be used afterwards.

Two figures put forward by the Region frame the procedure: more than **270,000 Brussels residents** are affected by this route, and **96%** of measured flights exceed Brussels noise standards.

### Extension beyond the Region

The challenge also continues on the Flemish side: forty-two families from Dilbeek, Lennik and Roosdaal are suing the Belgian State, and the municipality of **Lennik** is preparing its own action. The federal Mobility Minister maintains that this satellite-guided route is a European obligation and an alternative procedure when preferred runways cannot be used, notably because of wind.

No court has ruled on the merits at this stage.

## Jette in turn intervenes in the proceedings against RNP 07L (26 August 2026)

On the evening of **Wednesday 26 August 2026**, the municipality of **Jette** announced in a statement that it is intervening in **two ongoing proceedings** against the **RNP 07L** flight path: the **environmental cessation action** brought in 2018 by the Brussels Region, and the **challenge to Brussels Airport's environmental permit**.

This is the **execution of the motion adopted on 4 March 2026** by its municipal council, which already committed the commune to two legal actions. Jette thereby joins **Schaerbeek, Evere, Berchem-Sainte-Agathe** and, on the Flemish side, **Lennik**.

### A position that rejects the obvious remedy

What sets Jette's move apart is its content, not its form. The municipality writes: "**Simply scrapping RNP07 cannot on its own constitute a solution.**" The reasoning is that returning to the **VOR07 path would displace the nuisance rather than remove it**, which the Envisa study documented earlier in this dossier had already established for every alternative scenario.

Jette calls instead for the immediate introduction of a **cap limiting landings over the north-western municipalities**, together with noise-level and timing restrictions compatible with public health within the limits of aviation safety.

It is a rare position in this file: the municipality is challenging the route in court while saying publicly that winning would not be enough.

## What the route would cost, according to a residents' group (8 August 2026)

The **Free Air 4 Brussels** group published on 8 August 2026 an estimate of the cost of the RNP 07L path to the Brussels Region, which it puts at **four to six billion euros**.

**This figure is an activist estimate, not an official calculation. BGM publishes it only alongside its method and its limits.**

Breakdown announced by the group:

| Item | Estimated amount |
|---|---|
| Property depreciation | ~€3 billion |
| Regional and municipal tax losses | ~€766 million |
| Acoustic insulation of homes | ~€2 billion |

**The method.** The group applies to the **115,000 homes** and **280,000 residents** identified by the ULB study the results of a **2017 French study** on property transactions around **Roissy**, where the homes concerned lost in the order of **43,000 euros** in value. It describes its own projection as cautious.

**The limits, stated explicitly.** This is an extrapolation produced by a **party to the dispute**, based on a study covering **a different airport, a different property market and a different period**, nine years earlier. The three items cannot be added together without obvious double counting: an acoustically insulated home does not suffer the same discount as an untreated one.

**No authority has produced a counter-assessment or responded publicly to this costing.** No public estimate of the route's cost exists to date, which partly explains the attention this one has received.

Source: [BRUZZ (8 August 2026)](https://www.bruzz.be/actua/milieu/actiegroep-voorspelt-tot-6-miljard-euro-kosten-voor-brussels-gewest-door-overvliegroute-2026-08-08). Confidence: unconfirmed.

## The Superior Health Council's opinion calls for a ban on flights from 11pm to 7am (25 August 2026)

A feature published on 25 August 2026 gathers the state of knowledge on the health effects of air traffic and recalls the **opinion issued in 2024 by the Superior Health Council**, which recommends a **reduction in total air traffic** and a **complete ban on flights between 11pm and 7am**.

The effects documented in the literature cited concern first **sleep disturbance**, held to be the main causal factor, then cardiovascular and metabolic disease, anxiety and depressive disorders, and birth weight. A study around **Frankfurt airport** covering more than a thousand schoolchildren establishes that **ten additional decibels correspond to one month's delay in reading**, and twenty decibels to two months.

The researchers cited also note that the **ultrafine particles** emitted by air traffic pose a problem distinct from noise.
