## Open-ended strike notice in the regional public services from 1 October 2026 (29 September 2026)

On **Tuesday 29 September 2026**, according to La Libre, L'Avenir and La DH, **CGSP-ACOD** and **CSC-ACV** filed a **notice of open-ended strike and actions** covering all public services of the Brussels Region (sector XV, La Libre specifies), from **Thursday 1 October 2026**. The notice follows the end of consultation announced on Thursday 24 September 2026 by the minister for the Civil Service (see the next section). According to La Libre, the two unions announce actions without yet specifying how they will be carried out. The articles do not list the SLFP among the signatories.

The unions contest the savings measures targeting regional staff:

- the **strict hiring freeze in 2026** and the staff reduction targets over the legislative term; according to them, statements by the Brussels minister for the Budget and the Civil Service point to the removal of **at least 1,800 full-time equivalents by 2029**. The figure comes from the unions; the articles report no confirmation by the government;
- the draft amendment to the staff statute, which provides for the **removal of two days of leave** and an **annual evaluation**, both measures kept in the minister's final proposal (see the next section);
- according to L'Avenir and La DH, a lack of consistency in budget choices: management mandate posts are said to keep being opened while recruitment is at a standstill in 2026.

According to L'Avenir and La DH, the unions call the negotiation a “parody of negotiation”. None of the three articles reports a reaction from the government.

## Savings measures on regional staff: pay-scale cap withdrawn, consultation closed (23-24 September 2026)

After a second consultation meeting with the unions on **Wednesday 23 September 2026**, Minister for the Public Service Dirk De Smedt (Anders) presented his **final proposal** on the savings measures targeting staff of the regional administrations. In it he drops the **cap on pay seniority at 29 years**, which would have stopped staff pay-scale progression beyond that threshold. This is the minister's proposal, not a government decision.

Two measures remain in the proposal:

- the **removal of two days of leave**, 2 and 15 November; according to BX1 and the RTBF, staff were entitled to 35 days of leave as of 24 September 2026;
- an **annual assessment** of staff, instead of an assessment every two years.

On **Thursday 24 September 2026**, the minister announced the end of the consultation. The unions are divided: the **CSC** is asking for a mediator to be appointed, the **SLFP** agrees and the **CGSP** rejects the proposal.

## Telework in the regional civil service: IBSA measures its effects (September 2026)

**IBSA** (the Brussels Institute for Statistics and Analysis) published in September 2026 issue No. 8 of its "Evaluations", devoted to **telework in the Brussels civil service**. The quantitative analyses were carried out with the **Federal Planning Bureau**. The study covers the period 2014-2023 and draws in particular on staff data from six regional institutions and on statutory recruitment procedures.

- **Intensity.** The 2017 framework allowed up to three days of telework a week, but until 2020 this maximum applied to fewer than 10% of theoretically teleworkable posts. In 2023, nearly **75%** of staff in such a post were allowed to telework three days or more a week.
- **Recruitment.** Mentioning telework in a statutory job advertisement attracts on average **7 more applicants**, against an average of 101 applicants per advertisement, and **8 more applicants** pass CV screening, against an average of 60. IBSA notes that the confidence interval of the first result is relatively wide, although the effect remains statistically significant.
- **Retention.** A staff member allowed to telework would on average have **40.6% lower odds** of leaving their institution (odds ratio of 0.6026). No correlation appears among staff under 30, which IBSA suggests may be due to their shorter follow-up in the data.

IBSA makes four recommendations: formalise and harmonise the telework framework across institutions while preserving its flexibility; take account of its effects on attractiveness, well-being and retention in any change to telework policy; revitalise collective presence to limit the negative effects of intensive telework; reduce inequalities in ergonomics and allowances between staff.

The evaluation takes no position on the four-pillar reform or on the hiring freeze. It provides data on the attractiveness and retention of regional staff, while the hiring freeze has limited recruitment since December 2023 (see below).

Sources: [IBSA, news item (23 September 2026)](https://ibsa.brussels/actualites/les-evaluations-de-l-ibsa-ndeg8-le-teletravail-dans-la-fonction-publique-bruxelloise) ; [IBSA Evaluations No. 8 (September 2026, PDF)](https://ibsa.brussels/sites/default/files/publication/documents/Eval_IBSA_8_FR.pdf). Confidence: official.

## atlas.brussels: an opposition group questions the language split of the management mandates (22 September 2026)

On **Tuesday 22 September 2026**, the group leader of an opposition party (Ecolo) in the Brussels Parliament denounced a “political game” by the majority around the management mandates of **atlas.brussels**, the future Regional Support Service (see Pillar 1 below). According to her, quoted by L'Avenir (Belga wire), **six management mandates** are open, and it was decided, before the candidates were even known, which would be French-speaking and which Dutch-speaking: three and three, with a language role set for each post. ## Hiring freeze: more than 500 full-time equivalents fewer since January 2026 (12 September 2026)

According to the Brussels Minister for the Budget and the Public Service, quoted by BRUZZ on Saturday 12 September 2026, the Region's workforce has fallen by more than 500 full-time equivalents since the start of the year, 338 of them in the four months since the hiring freeze was tightened in March 2026. ## The announced "Big Bang": from ~25 entities to 4 pillars

The Regional Policy Declaration (DPR) of 13 February 2026 announces a major restructuring of the Brussels administration. The objective: to consolidate approximately 25 regional entities into **4 major pillars**, aiming for 250 to 300 million EUR in savings by 2029.

### Pillar 1 — Shared support service

Pooling of cross-cutting functions: human resources (talent.brussels), IT (Paradigm, Connect IT), finance and budget, statistics (IBSA), equal opportunities (Equal.brussels), simplification (Easy.brussels).

**Founding decree, 9 July 2026**: on Thursday 9 July 2026, the government of the Brussels-Capital Region adopted the decree creating the future Regional Support Service, together with the name of this future administration: **atlas.brussels**, for “Administration transversale de liaison et d'appui aux services” (cross-cutting administration for liaison and support to services). According to talent.brussels, this decree provides the legal framework needed to continue the implementation; the service's organisation, processes and working methods are still to be defined gradually. Source: [talent.brussels (9 July 2026)](https://www.talent.brussels/fr/actualites/atlasbrussels-le-futur-service-dappui-regional-devoile-son-nom-et-franchit-une-etape). Confidence: official.

### Pillar 2 — Core missions (SPRB)

Consolidation of policy directorates: Brussels International, Bruxelles Mobilite (administrative functions), Brussels Economy (with Innoviris, Screen.Brussels), Brussels Employment (integration of Actiris "under review"), Brussels Local Authorities, Brussels Housing (with Homegrade), **Brussels Urban Planning** (merger of perspective.brussels + urban.brussels — confirmed for 2026), Safe.Brussels.
Among these directorates, Brussels International absorbs the regional commissioner for Europe, whose post is to be abolished on 1 July 2026: see the [dedicated dossier](/en/dossiers/commissaire-europe).

### Pillar 3 — infrastructure.brussels

Public-law corporation bringing together operational services: Bruxelles Mobilite (construction, maintenance and DITP divisions), Port of Brussels (infrastructure), Bruxelles Environnement (operational services), Parking.brussels, LEZ management, ANPR cameras.

### Pillar 4 — Land platform

Coordination of land management: SAU (Urban Development Corporation), citydev.brussels, SLRB (social housing), Housing Fund, Port of Brussels (land concessions).

## Hiring freeze

The hiring freeze introduced in December 2023 by the outgoing government has been extended indefinitely:

- **2026**: general freeze (operational + non-operational); targeted recruitment remains possible where the service requires it, according to the Minister for the Public Service quoted by BRUZZ on 12 September 2026
- **From 2027**: freeze maintained except for operational functions
- **Mechanism**: no replacement of departures (retirements, resignations), internal mobility preferred
- **No layoffs** announced
- **Projected savings**: 50M EUR (2026), cumulating to 125M EUR (2029)
- Additional reduction of 25M EUR through the downsizing of ministerial cabinets

## A regional "pool de compétences" to absorb the hiring freeze (3 July 2026)

The Brussels Minister for the Public Service **Dirk De Smedt** (Anders) announced on **3 July 2026** the launch of a **"pool de compétences"** (Dutch: *competentiepool* — no official term has yet been announced in French, Dutch, English or German; BGM uses this working descriptive translation) intended to redirect staff from overstaffed administrations toward services facing a shortage of personnel.

The tool aims to structurally address the mismatch created by the **hiring freeze** (active since December 2023, see above): the freeze has reduced staff inflows but has not resolved the imbalance between administrations with surplus staff and those with genuine needs. Two tracks are planned:

- A **fast track**: volunteer staff go through a centralised matching procedure and can start, within 4 to 8 weeks, in a service with a concrete need, while keeping their status
- A **requalification track**, longer (6 to 18 months), for moves that require upskilling

**Social dialogue** on the project starts on **14 July 2026**, followed by a pilot phase to test the first tracks. Full operationalisation is targeted for **30 June 2027**. Trade unions are asking for prompt clarity on which administrations are affected.

Source: [BRUZZ (3 July 2026)](https://www.bruzz.be/actua/politiek/competentiepool-moet-helpen-om-met-personeel-te-schuiven-binnen-brusselse-administraties-2026-07-03). Confidence: unconfirmed (single source, no other outlet has picked up the information so far).

## First merger: perspective.brussels + urban.brussels (2026)

The merger of **perspective.brussels** (territorial development, 174 staff) and **urban.brussels** (urban planning and heritage) into a single entity called "Brussels Urban Planning" is the first concrete operation announced. It aims to create an **integrated permit** combining urban planning and environmental competences (currently split between Bruxelles Environnement and urban.brussels).

The case of **Actiris** (1,518 staff, headcount doubled in 20 years) has been identified as a potential integration into "Brussels Employment", but remains at the study stage.

## Decree approved in first reading (5 March 2026)

The Dilliès government approved on **5 March 2026**, in first reading, a decree creating a large transversal administration. This project concretises Pillar 1 (common support service) by consolidating transversal functions (HR, IT, finance, budget, statistics) under a single structure, placed under the authority of Minister of Public Service Dirk De Smedt (Anders).

This is the **first formal legislative act** of the 4-pillar reform.

Sources: Le Soir, BRUZZ (5 March 2026).

**Second reading — 5 June 2026**: The Brussels government approved the second reading of the first pillar (shared support service). More than a hundred civil servants from different administrations took part in the preparation; the government intends to permanently anchor the new structure by the summer of 2026. Next step: submission to the Brussels Parliament.

**Founding decree, 9 July 2026**: the government adopted the decree creating the future Regional Support Service, named atlas.brussels (see Pillar 1 above).

## Consultancy in the crosshairs

Cutting consultancy fees is one of the savings levers the government is putting forward: **up to EUR 67 million a year** would be saved on this item by 2029, within a EUR 281 million package of "operational resource optimisation".

The issue is long-documented. According to openbudgets.brussels data relayed by the press, Brussels public services spent **at least EUR 107 million on private audit and consulting firms over 2018-2022**, of which roughly **EUR 50 million for Deloitte alone** (ahead of KPMG, EY and PwC). A study by the PTB put the 2019-2024 total at around EUR 470 million.

In June 2026, a press investigation (Le Vif) found that reliance on IT consultancy was continuing — over EUR 300,000 earmarked for 1.5 full-time equivalents over a year in a regional contract — illustrating the difficulty of meeting the announced trajectory. *(Press data, single-source, to be corroborated via public tenders.)*

## Workforce trajectory: 243 staff (1989) to 12,000 (2024)

The Brussels regional administration has experienced continuous growth since the creation of the Region in 1989:

- **1989**: 243 staff at the Ministry of the Brussels-Capital Region (MRBC)
- **2015**: 8,756 regional civil servants
- **2023**: 12,093 civil servants (+37% in 8 years)
- **Wage bill** (incl. STIB-MIVB): from 1.2 bn EUR (2018) to 1.8 bn EUR (2024), i.e. +50%
- **Ratio**: 1 regional civil servant per 100 residents (vs 1/280 in Flanders and Wallonia)

This growth is explained by successive transfers of competences (6th Reform of the State, 2014), the dissolution of the Province of Brabant (1993), and the continuous creation of new bodies: visit.brussels, Innoviris, parking.brussels, perspective.brussels, hub.brussels, etc.

## Financial context: adverse opinion from the Court of Auditors

The 30th Book of Observations by the Court of Auditors (November 2025) issued an **adverse opinion** on the Region's 2024 accounts, identifying "significant anomalies with a diffuse impact" across the 21 autonomous administrative bodies. Key figures:

- **Actual deficit**: 1.65 bn EUR (31.5% of revenues)
- **Consolidated gross debt**: 15.6 bn EUR (+76.7% over 2020-2024)
- **Interest charges**: from 91M EUR (2016) to 399M EUR (2024), i.e. x4.4

## Documented scepticism

Several experts and institutional actors have expressed reservations about the feasibility of the reform:

- **Prof. Marie Goransson (ULB)**: "Even with all of this, you still don't reach 500 million in savings." She estimates that without layoffs, savings depend on natural attrition — a slow and uncertain process.
- **CSC (trade union)**: questions about the physical consolidation of buildings and transitional protections for staff.
- **Voka**: "The agreement contains the right ingredients, but questions remain about the budgetary trajectory."

## perspective.brussels leadership: mandate vacant since the announced merger (June-July 2026)

The Director-General mandate at **perspective.brussels** (Antoine de Borman) has expired without the Brussels government deciding on its renewal, precisely because of the announced merger with urban.brussels (see Pillar 2 above). On **11 June 2026**, a staff gathering voiced concern over this leadership vacancy and the broader uncertainty surrounding the reform. The Secretary of State in charge of the dossier, **Audrey Henry**, acknowledged the concerns and promised consultations, without announcing a decision.

**Why flag this**: the mechanism is the same one that led to the abolition of the regional commissioner to Europe post (see [dedicated dossier](/en/dossiers/commissaire-europe)) — institutional simplification makes leadership mandates redundant once two entities merge. But unlike the commissioner-to-Europe case, **no abolition decision has been officially announced at this stage**: this is a frozen/vacant mandate pending the merger, not a scrapped position. BGM classifies this as "developing," not to be confused with a closed case.
