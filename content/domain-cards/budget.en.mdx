## 2027 budget: government agreement, targeted deficit of 719 million euros (4 October 2026)

The Brussels government reached an agreement on the 2027 budget on the evening of **Sunday 4 October 2026**, after a two-day conclave. The deficit is to be brought down to **719 million euros in 2027**, from 957 million in 2026, with a return to balance in **2029**. According to the Minister-President's office, quoted by Le Soir, the trajectory foresees a deficit of 416 million euros in 2028. The 2026 budget was adjusted in the same agreement, according to BRUZZ.

Before Parliament on Monday 5 October 2026, the Minister-President said the government had started from a deficit of more than 1.3 billion euros and had now halved it (remarks reported by L'Avenir and VRT NWS). The starting amounts quoted vary from one statement to another: 1.6 billion euros in 2025 in the government's press release, 1.552 billion in 2024 according to the Minister-President's office.

Measures announced by members of the government and reported by several newsrooms:

- **Civil service**: the hiring freeze is maintained in 2027 and ten administrations join atlas.brussels on 1 January 2027 (see the [Administrative reform](/en/dossiers/reforme-administration) dossier). The expected saving on staff is 70 million euros according to BRUZZ, 74 million a year according to VRT NWS.
- **Taxation**: the Be Home premium is doubled and the regional surcharge on personal income tax falls by one point, two measures already included in the government agreement.
- **Road tax**: small city cars and zero-emission vehicles are exempt from the second half of 2027 (see the [Mobility](/en/domains/mobility) card).
- **Renovation**: zero-interest loans replace the Renolution grants in spring 2027, with 66 million euros a year (see the [Climate](/en/domains/climate) card).
- **Property**: the Minister-President's official residence, rue Ducale, will be sold. According to his spokesperson, quoted by BRUZZ, the sale would raise between 10 and 20 million euros and take place during the following legislature.
- **Social**: the price of service vouchers and family allowances do not change; an additional 15 million euros go to municipalities and CPAS in 2027 (see the [Social](/en/domains/social) card).

Two points remain open on 5 October 2026. The **SLRB's 131 million euros**: the Socialist ministers and the Budget minister say these funds are confirmed, the Minister-President states that the matter was not dealt with (see the [Housing](/en/domains/housing) card). The budget of the **Common Community Commission**: an agreement was found on Sunday shortly before midnight, according to La Libre.

The budget tables were not given to members of parliament before the statement. The Minister-President undertook to send the multi-year table before the debate of Tuesday 6 October 2026, according to L'Avenir.

## The Federal Planning Bureau sees a high risk of unsustainable regional debt (15 September 2026)

The **Federal Planning Bureau** rates as **high** the risk that the public debt of the **Brussels Region** becomes unsustainable in the medium term, on the basis of the analysis of several scenarios. It reaches the same finding for the **Walloon Region** and the **French Community**. In the reference scenario, the **Flemish Community** is the only entity whose risk is rated **low**.

The analysis recalls that total Belgian public debt reached **692 billion euros** at the end of 2025, or **108%** of gross domestic product, and that the share of the federated entities in that debt rose from **8.4% in 2008** to **18.4% in 2025**.

The debt ratio is expressed as a percentage of the entity's **adjusted available revenue (RDA)**: its total revenue, less the transfers it pays to other levels of government and stripped of notional ESA revenue. In the reference scenario, the Brussels debt ratio reaches **328%** of RDA in **2031**, and the probability that 2031 debt exceeds 2026 debt is **100%**.

For comparison, the same exercise puts the Walloon Region at **327%** of RDA in 2031 (probability of **99%**), the French Community at **158%** (**99%**), and the Flemish Community at **118%** (**95%**). The **fiscal consolidation space**, which measures the room an entity has to correct its trajectory, stands at **18%** for Brussels, against **36%** for Wallonia and **34%** for Flanders; it is not computed for the French Community.

Several newsrooms described the Flemish risk as “moderate”. That is the Flemish Community's rating in the “historical primary balance” scenario, not in the reference scenario, where it is **low**.

Sources: [Federal Planning Bureau, “Debt Sustainability Analysis (DSA) applied to the federated entities”, doc. ## City of Brussels: the surcharge rises from 2,950 to 3,457, and the regional premium acts as a buffer (17 August 2026)

The **2026 property tax** assessment notices of the City of Brussels went out from **17 August 2026**. The **municipal surcharge** rises from **2,950 to 3,457**, an increase of **17.2%**.

The City announces that this increase is **fully and automatically offset** for owners who live in their property, through two cumulative mechanisms: a **flat municipal deduction of one hundred euros** and the **regional Be-Home premium**, **164 euros in 2026** and **328 euros in 2027**. Owners of several properties pay on average around thirty euros more per month.

### What the arrangement reveals

The doubling of the Be-Home premium is a **regional commitment** written into the policy declaration and tracked on this card. In the City of Brussels it does not translate into a net saving for the owner-occupier: it **absorbs an increase decided by the municipality**. The regional taxpayer thus funds the buffer for a municipal tax decision, and the owner-occupier is held at break-even rather than relieved.

The move is not isolated: nine municipalities raised their surcharge in 2026, the largest increase being in Saint-Josse-ten-Noode, from 2,980 to 4,090.

## Summer agreement: income tax confirmed, consultant refused (16-17 July 2026)

Meeting at **Val Duchesse on 16 July 2026** for its last session before the summer recess, the Brussels government confirmed the **one-point cut in personal income tax**, applicable from **tax year 2028** (2027 income). Announced budgetary cost: **EUR 58 million per year**, of which around EUR 3 million borne by municipalities, a loss the government announced it would compensate. According to the Budget cabinet, the relief would amount to around EUR 147 per year for a single person without children on a net monthly salary of EUR 2,700, and around EUR 152 per year for a married couple with two children each earning EUR 2,400 net per month, figures lower than the initial estimate of ~EUR 268/year communicated at the time of the February coalition agreement.

**A criticised and refused consultant hire.** During the same period, several MPs criticised in plenary session the hiring, by the cabinet of the Minister of Finance and Budget, of an IT consultant billed at **EUR 1,258 including VAT per day**, for a total cost of **EUR 818,320** over 2026-2029. The cabinet justified the choice as the cheapest option between two existing framework contracts, necessary to unify six IT institutions within the future support service **Atlas.brussels**. According to BX1 and BRUZZ, the Brussels government **refused or postponed** this hire in mid-July 2026, despite a favourable opinion from the Finance Inspectorate. This episode stands in direct tension with the commitment made in February 2026 to "reduce reliance on external consultants" (see below).

## First budget measures (February 2026)

From the start of work on 15 February, the government announced the first austerity measures, which contributed to bringing the deficit down to EUR 957 million in the 2026 budget:

- **Extension of the hiring freeze** (in effect since late 2023) in the regional civil service, for the full legislature (no layoffs)
- **Reduction of external consultant use**
- **Cuts to discretionary subsidies** — systematic review of all funding envelopes
- **Target**: deficit below EUR 1 billion in 2026, return to balanced budget by 2029

## Coalition Agreement: Announced Commitments
The agreement concluded on 12 February 2026 between the seven parties of the new Brussels majority sets an ambitious budgetary framework:

- **Return to a balanced budget by 2029** — trajectory of ~EUR 1.2 billion in savings, split 80% spending cuts and 20% new revenue
- **No new taxes** — no City Tax, no kilometre charge. The existing regional tax on tourist accommodation establishments was nevertheless raised by an ordinance of 17 July 2025, voted during the previous legislature and applicable from the 2026 tax year: from 4 to 5 euros per accommodation unit per night at the standard rate, municipal surcharges included, and from 3 to 4 euros for homestays ([Justel](https://www.ejustice.just.fgov.be/cgi_loi/article.pl?language=fr&lg_txt=f&cn_search=2025071703); [be.brussels, 9 December 2025](https://be.brussels/en/taxes-finances/tax-tourist-accommodation-establishments-higher-rates-1-january-2026)).
- **One-point reduction in regional personal income tax** — estimated gain of ~EUR 268/year per Brussels worker
- **Registration duties** — exemption threshold raised from EUR 600,000 to EUR 800,000
- **Administrative reform** — merger of 25 regional structures into 4 entities (4 pillars) with a 20 to 30% reduction in operational costs
- **Extension of the hiring freeze** (in effect since late 2023) for the full legislature (no layoffs)
- **Discretionary subsidies** — systematic review of all funding envelopes

The government received the confidence vote of Parliament on 27 February 2026. The implementation of these commitments is now being realised through the 2026 budget, approved on 5 March and filed to Parliament on 6 March 2026.

### Doubling of the Be Home premium (18 June 2026)

On 18 June 2026, the government gave first-reading approval to **doubling the Be Home premium**, a tax reduction for owners who occupy their own home, which will rise from **164 to 328 euros per year** in 2027. Around **200,000** Brussels households benefit from it. The measure represents additional fiscal expenditure for the Region and falls within the budgetary trajectory to be monitored (target of a balanced budget by 2029). The text now goes for opinion to the Council of State and the social partners before a second reading. The housing dimension is detailed in the Housing card.

### Property tax reduction for children: a flat amount from 2027 (study of 1 October 2026)

The draft ordinance approved at first reading on 18 June 2026 does more than double the Be Home premium: it also replaces with a **flat amount** the property tax (précompte immobilier) reduction granted to families (10% per child from two children) and to households that include a person with a disability (20%), from **tax year 2027**.

According to a study by the Ligue des familles published on 1 October 2026, the flat amount has not yet been set. The Finance minister's cabinet mentions as a working assumption the amounts applied in Wallonia, namely **125 euros per child** and **250 euros in the event of disability**. The current budget for these reductions is **33 million euros a year**, and the expected saving **9 million euros a year** from 2027, or 27%. It is meant to fund family allowances, which fall under the Common Community Commission. In 2025, **54,361 reductions** were granted to households with at least two children, for **436.33 euros on average**. With the amounts mentioned, any family whose property tax exceeds **1,250 euros** would lose out, according to the study. The vote in Parliament is expected in October 2026.

## Municipal finances: EUR 1.718 billion in transferred charges (March 2026)

Alongside the regional budget, the **19 Brussels municipalities** face their own financial shock. On 11 March 2026, Brulocalis quantified at **EUR 1.718 billion** (2025-2029) the additional charges imposed on municipalities by decisions of other levels of government. The four main items are statutory staff pensions, police zone funding, the tax reform (EUR -17M/year in municipal revenue from 2029) and the influx to CPAS linked to the unemployment reform. ## Finance Committee: budget approved (23 March 2026)

The **Finance Committee** of the Brussels Parliament approved the regional 2026 budget on **23 March**, with the majority voting against the opposition. The DPR commitment has been met: the deficit remains **under the promised one billion**.

**Key figures for the 2026 budget:**

- **Revenue**: EUR 6.622 billion
- **Expenditure**: EUR 7.613 billion
- **Deficit**: EUR 957 million (down from ~EUR 1.2 billion in 2025)

The deficit partly relies on the **"code 8"** mechanism: approximately **1 billion EUR** in capital injections (Vivaqua, NEO, Kanal, SLRB) is excluded from the deficit calculation, recorded as equity participation. **Bruxelles-Proprete** receives a budget of **EUR 315 million** (+EUR 2 million compared to 2025), plus **EUR 28 million** dedicated to security and cleanliness at the **Midi station**.

## Plenary vote: 2026 budget adopted (27 March 2026)

The Brussels Parliament adopted the 2026 regional budget on **27 March 2026** by a vote of **53 for and 32 against**, following a week of intensive deliberations. This vote marks the **definitive end of the provisional twelfths regime** that had been in force for the Region since late 2024.

**Key elements:**

## A second 250-million-euro credit line, this time with BBVA (26 August 2026)

On **Wednesday 26 August 2026** the **Finance Minister** announced the opening of a **250-million-euro credit line with Banco Bilbao Vizcaya Argentaria (BBVA)**. The stated aim is to strengthen the Region's liquidity buffers and further diversify its funding base.

### Two lines of the same size, not to be confused

This line **comes on top of** those already in place. It is **not the same** as the 250-million-euro line negotiated with **Belfius** under the cashier contract announced on 20 July 2026, which raised the available ceiling from 50 to 250 million euros. These are two distinct instruments, with two different institutions, for an identical amount.

Following this operation the Region holds **1.25 billion euros of credit lines** across **four providers**:

| Provider | Line |
|---|---|
| Deutsche Bank | (amount not disclosed) |
| ING | (amount not disclosed) |
| Belfius | €250m |
| BBVA | €250m |

The breakdown is not published for the first two: only the 1.25 billion total and the two 250-million lines are known.

### What this measures, and what it does not

A credit line is a **drawing capacity**, not debt. It does not change the Region's outstanding debt until it is used, and **no drawn amount has been disclosed**. What the operation documents is therefore the build-up of a liquidity reserve and a widening of the lender pool, not a deterioration or improvement in the budget position.
