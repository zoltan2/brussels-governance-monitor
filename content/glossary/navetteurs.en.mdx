Commuters (French: navetteurs) are workers who travel daily to Brussels from Flanders or Wallonia to work there without residing in the Region. In 2024, approximately **400,000** people living in Flanders or Wallonia worked in the Brussels-Capital Region, out of 809,900 jobs, compared with 410,100 Brussels residents working in their own Region (Statbel Labour Force Survey, published by IBSA). Commuters therefore hold close to half of all jobs in Brussels, roughly as many as Brussels residents themselves.

This phenomenon is at the heart of the **Brussels fiscal paradox**: Brussels produced 17.3% of Belgian GDP in 2023 (IBSA), but the personal income tax (IPP) of commuters is located at their tax domicile (Special Financing Law of 16 January 1989, art. 5/1, § 2). The regional share of this tax and the municipal surcharge therefore go to the Region and the municipality where they live, outside Brussels. Brussels bears the infrastructure costs (roads, transport, security, sanitation) without receiving the corresponding tax revenues.

This imbalance partly explains why Brussels, despite its apparent economic wealth, has lower public revenue per capita than the other two Regions. The question of Brussels' financing and the fair distribution of costs related to commuters is a recurring issue in Belgian institutional negotiations.
