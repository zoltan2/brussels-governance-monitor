## Context

The regional and federal elections of 9 June 2024 opened a period of government formation in all three Regions. The speed of formation directly determined each Region's ability to adopt a full budget.

## Brussels-Capital: the budget deadlock

The Brussels-Capital Region remained without a fully empowered government until 14 February 2026. During that period, the outgoing government handled current affairs and the Brussels Parliament voted provisional appropriation ordinances (provisional twelfths). The 2026 budget, voted in plenary on 27 March 2026, ended this regime.

**Concrete consequences of the provisional regime:**
- No new investment could be launched
- Subsidies and multi-year agreements reaching expiry could not be renewed
- Cumulative inflation (~6% over two years) eroded the real value of expenditure

## Flanders: swift return to budget normality

The Flemish government was sworn in on 30 September 2024, nearly four months after the elections. The Flemish Parliament voted the 2025 budget on 20 December 2024, allowing investments and regional policies to continue without significant interruption.

## Wallonia: the fastest formation

The Walloon government was sworn in on 15 July 2024, five weeks after the elections. The Walloon Parliament voted the 2025 budget on 18 December 2024, before the start of the financial year.

## What the comparison reveals

The divergence between the three Regions illustrates a phenomenon specific to Brussels: institutional complexity (linguistic balances, number of parties needed to form a majority) considerably lengthens the formation process. Flanders and Wallonia voted their 2025 budgets before the start of the financial year; Brussels operated under an exceptional regime until its 2026 budget was voted in March 2026.
