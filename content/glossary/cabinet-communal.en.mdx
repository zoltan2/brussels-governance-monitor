A cabinet is a political tool, not an administrative one. Its members work for an elected official and their assignment ends with that official's term, whereas municipal staff hold a statutory position and serve the commune whatever the majority.

**The distinction that matters when reading the figures.** A municipal council sets an **authorised establishment**, that is a ceiling of posts. That ceiling does not say how many posts are actually filled. When a commune announces a number of full-time equivalents actually employed, that figure is a different measure and does not compare with a neighbouring commune's establishment.

**The levels.** A cabinet post is graded from level A, which assumes a university degree, down to level E. A total of posts therefore adds up very unequal roles: a driver at level D counts as one unit just as a cabinet director at level A9 does. That is why the City of Brussels adopted a points system weighting each grade, rather than a ceiling expressed in people.
