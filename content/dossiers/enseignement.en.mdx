## Why this dossier directly concerns Brussels residents

In Brussels, your child's school is managed by the **Wallonia-Brussels Federation** (if it is francophone) or by the **Flemish Community** (if it is Dutch-speaking). The **Brussels-Capital Region** — to which your family pays regional taxes — has virtually no power over compulsory education. This is the first anomaly of the "institutional lasagna" for Brussels parents: the Brussels government cannot prevent cuts decided in central Brussels at the FWB Parliament, nor those decided at the Flemish Parliament.

**How many children in each network?** According to perspective.brussels (Panorama of school in Brussels, 2023-2024), **92%** of Brussels children aged 3 to 17 attend a school organised or subsidised by one of the Communities (FWB or Flemish Community); the remaining 8% attend private, European or international schools, or are home-schooled. **Dutch-language education** in Brussels counts around **53,600 students** in 2024-2025 — about **one child in five** enrolled in the Community networks (VGC / GO! Scholengroep Brussel). The vast majority of the others attend the FWB network.

**How many households combine both networks?** According to IBSA (data as of 1 January 2021, published in August 2025), among 128,903 Brussels households with at least one child aged 3-17:
- **96% (124,013 households)** enrol their children in a **single** system;
- **2.5% (2,897 households)** combine FWB and Flemish Community;
- **1% (1,286)** FWB + another system (private, European, international);
- **0.5% (660)** Flemish + another system.

In 2026, these two levels of power — over which the Brussels Region has no leverage — are simultaneously imposing cuts that affect:
- Brussels teachers and school heads (secondary, primary, special needs);
- francophone students (end of free schooling in P1-P3, tuition increase);
- Dutch-speaking newcomer students (reduction in OKAN coach funding, see dedicated section below);
- higher education students (+€359 in tuition per year, FWB side).

## FWB section (francophone education)

### Start of the 2026 school year: the meeting with the minister breaks down (1 September 2026)

On **Tuesday 1 September 2026**, the Education Minister of the Wallonia-Brussels Federation received the trade unions to try to restart dialogue after months of protest. **CSC-Enseignement, CGSP-Enseignement and SETCa-SEL left the table after about an hour.**

According to the three organisations, the minister offered only the creation of an **advisory committee** to improve consultation with the sector, **without revisiting the contested measures**. The unions set two conditions for the discussion: a **refinancing of the Wallonia-Brussels Federation by the federal government**, or a **withdrawal of the austerity measures**. With neither on the table, they left. "We wasted our time," one of them summed up.

Useful context is a statement of **23 August 2026**: the minister then said she no longer wished to propose savings in education before 2029. That commitment covers **future** measures, not those **already decided**, which explains why it did not suffice.

The unions are calling to step up the movement. A **demonstration is set for Thursday 10 September 2026 in Liège**, with a strike notice filed since August.

**What changes for a Brussels parent**: nothing in the immediate term. The contested measures, described further down in this section, remain in force for the 2026-2027 school year. The Brussels Region has no leverage over this budget.

The **Mars Attacks** collective, described below as grouping around forty-five establishments in March 2026, met in Liège on 1 September. **Le Soir writes that it brings together "nearly 390 schools."** We reproduce that figure in quotation marks and do not establish it: it comes from **a single newsroom, on a single date**, it is corroborated by no other source, the collective publishes no membership list, and it would represent a jump from forty-five to three hundred and ninety establishments in five months. We also do not know whether "school" here means a site or an organising body. This figure feeds no metric in this dossier.

### The Glatigny budget plan: €86.7M in compulsory education (2026)

The Wallonia-Brussels Federation government, led by Education Minister **Valérie Glatigny (MR)**, announced in October 2025 a savings plan of **€500 million** for the 2026-2029 period, including **€255 million in 2026**. Compulsory education bears the heaviest burden with **€86.7 million** in cuts the first year.

The concrete measures for the 2026-2027 school year:

- **Teachers' working time**: upper secondary teachers move from 20 to **22 hours of classroom teaching per week**, aligning their workload with lower secondary. For a Brussels school, this reform **mechanically reduces the number of temporary positions** (fewer remaining hours to distribute).
- **Sick leave**: after exhausting sick days, the maintained rate drops from 80-70% to **60%** — a loss of purchasing power for already-precarious teachers.
- **Operating funds**: school budgets are **not indexed** in 2026 — in practice, a real decrease.
- **Free primary schooling**: abolition of free schooling for grades **P1 to P3**, redirected to school budgets with "modest savings".
- **Teacher status**: the **permanent appointment** is replaced by a new "CDI-E" contract (school-model permanent contract) from **2027**. New teachers will no longer benefit from the historic job security.
- **Higher education**: catch-up of tuition indexation since 2011 — the standard rate rises from **€835 to €1,194 per year** from the start of the 2026-2027 academic year, a +43% increase. For Brussels, this directly affects families of students at ULB, VUB (via its FWB component), USL-B and francophone University Colleges.

The FWB government claims to have "protected" current teachers' salaries, indexation for serving teachers, class sizes and the reform of initial teacher training. It also announces a **5% increase** in the salary of new master-degree qualified teachers from 2027.

## Flemish Community section (Dutch-language education)

### The Brussels premium for teachers: seven years of demands, still no decision (27 August to 1 September 2026)

On **Thursday 27 August 2026**, at the Koekelberg Basilica, the Flemish Education Minister closed the door on a **Brusselpremie**, a bonus for teachers working in Dutch-language education in Brussels: "that is not the solution, we studied it, but abroad such a system was tried and it turned out not to work." She announces other measures, focused on the big cities including Brussels, which she hopes to land in 2026. Among the avenues mentioned: first-class rail passes and reimbursement of parking cards for commuting teachers.

On **31 August and 1 September**, the Brussels minister for Dutch-language education calls the refusal "short-sighted"; the education umbrella organisations, teachers and a VUB academic also contest it.

#### Two schemes under the same figure

The figure of five hundred euros circulates for two different things, and the confusion is constant:

- the **proposed Brusselpremie**, "at least five hundred euros net" for teachers working in Brussels. That figure comes from a **2024 election debate**, not from any official costing;
- a **referral bonus already paid** by a Brussels school group since **28 August 2024**: two hundred and fifty euros to a staff member who brings in a teacher, and two hundred and fifty more if the recruit is still in post after eighteen months.

#### A seven-year-old demand

It has been publicly pressed since **May 2019** by figures in Brussels Dutch-language education, including one of the officials quoted again in 2026. No official amount has ever been costed, and **no payer has ever been designated**: the Brussels minister contesting the refusal does not say who would fund it, how much, or on what timetable.

#### The state of the shortage

Administrative data from the Flemish Department of Education, as at October 2024:

| Indicator | Value |
|---|---|
| Teachers missing in Brussels | **459** (293 primary, 166 secondary) |
| Unfilled vacancies | **10%** in primary, **7%** in secondary |
| Shortfall across all Dutch-language education | **3,700 FTE**, around 3.7% |
| Pupils concerned in Brussels (2024-2025) | **53,613** |
| Brussels teachers holding the required qualification (2023-2024) | **59%**, against 75% in Flanders and 72% in Brussels in 2016 |

The last indicator is the most telling: this is not only a problem of numbers but of **qualification**, and it has worsened by **thirteen points in seven years**.

#### Two sets of figures, two instruments

The **VGC** published press releases in February and October 2024 reporting a **fall** in the shortage, based on a **declarative survey of school organising bodies**, under the previous minister: open posts down from 227 to 209, then 231 vacancies and one school in three with no vacancy. The figures placing Brussels at the top come from the **Flemish Department**, based on **administrative data**. Two hundred and thirty-one vacancies on one side, four hundred and fifty-nine missing teachers on the other: these are not the same objects, and there is no contradiction, but two measuring instruments.

#### The police precedent, invoked and turned around

The argument for the bonus is that it "worked well for the police." An allowance does indeed exist, provided for by the **Royal Decree of 30 March 2001** on the legal position of police service personnel, **articles XI.III.28 to 30 and annex 7**, amended by the **Royal Decree of 16 June 2009**. It rises with seniority on the Region's territory, from **669.32 euros in the first year to 1,338.63 euros from the sixth**, in non-indexed annual base amounts, the top step requiring a five-year commitment. The amount actually paid is higher, since the allowance is index-linked. **BGM does not publish an indexed amount for 2026: the official SSGPI sheet stops at 1 January 2013.** As an order of magnitude, BRUZZ put the allowance at around two hundred euros gross per month in 2021.

Yet on **14 October 2021** that same outlet ran the headline that the allowance "must be put in perspective": a police union judged its effect negligible, notably for lack of a mileage allowance for travelling to work in Brussels, and the HR director of a Brussels zone stated "we therefore do not put that allowance front and centre." The zones then had one hundred and sixty vacancies out of one thousand and ten for one, one hundred out of two thousand seven hundred for the other.

The precedent invoked to show that a bonus works is therefore one in which **those concerned themselves qualify its effect**. That does not settle the education debate, but it shifts the burden of proof.

#### Nobody cites a study

The Flemish minister invokes foreign experience **without naming it**. A VUB professor asserts the opposite, citing foreign evidence of positive effects on attraction and retention, **also without a reference**. BGM found no published study supporting either position.

#### Who decides

The bonus falls to the **Flemish Community**. The VGC and the Brussels minister call for it, the **Brussels Region has no competence**, and the budget is voted in the Flemish Parliament. The contrast with the police is the key: two shortage occupations in the same city, two opposite answers depending on which level of power is the employer. The Brussels police officer has a Brussels allowance because the employer is federal and local; the Dutch-speaking teacher has none because the employer is Flemish.

Sources: [BRUZZ (27 Aug. 2026)](https://www.bruzz.be/actua/onderwijs/demir-gooit-deur-dicht-geen-brusselpremie-wel-andere-maatregelen-tegen-lerarentekort-2026-08-27); [BRUZZ (31 Aug. 2026)](https://www.bruzz.be/actua/onderwijs/de-smedt-demir-veegt-brusselpremie-te-snel-van-tafel-2026-08-31); [BRUZZ (31 Aug. 2026)](https://www.bruzz.be/actua/onderwijs/leraren-politici-en-onderwijsexperts-brusselpremie-kan-wel-helpen-tegen-lerarentekort-2026-08-31); [BRUZZ (14 Oct. 2021, police allowance)](https://www.bruzz.be/veiligheid/brusselse-politiekorpsen-strijden-om-rekruten-brusselpremie-moet-gerelativeerd-worden); [VRT NWS (28 Aug. 2024, referral bonus)](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2024/08/28/go-lanceert-premie-van-250-euro-voor-personeelsleden-die-een-ler/); [Onderwijs in Brussel, figures](https://www.onderwijsinbrussel.be/pers/cijfers-over-het-nederlandstalig-onderwijs-brussel); SSGPI, financial personnel administration manual, version of 2 December 2024. ## The double lasagna: who decides what for your child's school?

| Level | Competence over a Brussels child's school |
|---|---|
| **Wallonia-Brussels Federation** | All francophone education: curricula, funding, teacher status, exams, free schooling |
| **Flemish Community** | All Dutch-language education: curricula, funding, teacher status, exams |
| **COCOF** | Francophone vocational training in Brussels, some peripheral arrangements |
| **VGC** | Dutch-language school infrastructure, pedagogical support (Onderwijscentrum Brussel), out-of-school care, libraries |
| **Brussels municipalities** | Municipal school network, building maintenance, out-of-school care, canteens |
| **Brussels Region** | **No direct competence** over compulsory education. Can coordinate (Masterplan Tweetaligheid, vocational training via Bruxelles Formation), fund renovations via Beliris, but cannot hire teachers, define curricula, or compensate for cuts decided elsewhere. |

This fragmentation explains why **no Brussels regional decision** can cancel or mitigate cuts decided in central Brussels at the FWB Parliament (rue de la Loi) or at the Flemish Parliament (rue du Gouvernement provisoire). A Brussels parent wanting to defend their child's school must address the right level of power — information that precarious families, or non-francophone/non-Dutch-speaking families, have the most difficulty obtaining.

## Regional commitment (RPD 2026): the Masterplan Tweetaligheid

The Brussels Regional Policy Declaration of 12 February 2026 provides for a **Masterplan Tweetaligheid** (bilingualism), but it concerns only:
- the training of regional civil servants;
- municipal services (functional bilingualism);
- Brussels hospitals.

It **does not cover** compulsory education, which remains an exclusive community competence. Brussels residents therefore remain dependent on the budget choices of the FWB and the Flemish Community for their children's schools.
