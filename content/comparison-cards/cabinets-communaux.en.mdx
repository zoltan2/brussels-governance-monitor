## Context

Article 21bis of the New Municipal Act leaves each municipal council free to decide whether the members of its college have a cabinet, and to set its composition. That freedom produces nineteen different answers across a territory of 162 square kilometres. Brussels Local Authorities gathered those decisions into a single survey, as at 25 September 2025, which makes comparison possible for the first time.

## The data compared

The range runs from zero to twenty-three. **Auderghem** is the only commune to grant no cabinet to anyone. At the other end, **Ixelles** authorises twenty-three posts and **Schaerbeek** 19.5, allocated by political group.

Set against population, the picture shifts. **Saint-Josse-ten-Noode** authorises one post per roughly 2,100 inhabitants, **Ixelles** one per 3,900. Conversely, **Evere** and **Uccle** sit above 21,000 inhabitants per post, ten times fewer resources relative to population. The two did not make the same choice, however: Uccle reserves its four posts for the mayor alone, Evere places two staff at the disposal of the whole college.

## Three models, not nineteen

Behind the spread of figures, the survey reveals three ways of doing things.

**No cabinet.** Auderghem, alone.

**The mayor only.** Uccle and Berchem-Sainte-Agathe, and but for one alderwoman Ganshoren. Between two and four people, with aldermen having no staff of their own.

**A distributed establishment.** The rest, with two rationalising mechanisms visible in the detail. **Pooling** between aldermen of the same group, in Jette and Woluwe-Saint-Pierre, where staff are allocated per group of three or four aldermen rather than individually. And allocation **by electoral list** rather than by person, in Anderlecht, Saint-Gilles, Schaerbeek and Watermael-Boitsfort, which moves the arbitration from the college to the parties.

The City of Brussels is the only case of a **points system**: each grade consumes a number of points, and the cap applies to the weighted total rather than to a headcount. A cabinet can therefore employ more staff by recruiting at a lower level. That mechanism, adopted in early 2025, explains the growth in headcount observed there in August 2026.

## One trajectory, only one

Schaerbeek is the only commune in the survey to write an evolution over time into its decision: its establishment goes from 19.5 posts for 2025-2028 to 18.5 for 2028-2030. Everywhere else the establishment is fixed for the term.

## What the source does not say

The survey gives a ceiling, not a reality. It says neither how many posts are actually filled nor what they cost. It contains no budget data, global or per commune. That is the main limit of this comparison: it measures what the communes allowed themselves, not what they do.
