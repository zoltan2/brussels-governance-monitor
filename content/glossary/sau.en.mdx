The SAU is neither a private developer nor an administration. It is the operator through which the Region acquires, assembles and prepares land, then steers the projects built on it: housing, public facilities, neighbourhood redevelopment. It holds a right of pre-emption.

It works on six strategic sites: Usquare in Ixelles, Mediapark in Schaerbeek, Josaphat between Schaerbeek and Evere, the Anderlecht Abattoirs, the Uccle-Boitsfort Hippodrome, and the North Quarter and the Herrmann-Debroux axis. It also delivers regional facilities such as the new fire brigade headquarters, the safe.brussels centre, the Cureghem barracks, Magasin 4 and the Cat Museum. The team numbers twenty-three people according to the 2024 activity report.

Why this term matters when reading the news: when a permit application is filed on a Brussels strategic site, the applicant is often the SAU, that is, the Region itself acting as operator. Mistaking the SAU for a private developer changes the reading of a file such as the Josaphat wasteland entirely.
