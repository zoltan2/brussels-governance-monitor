## What is the ACS scheme?

**Agents Contractuels Subventionnés** (ACS, Subsidised Contract Workers) are the main employment support mechanism for the Brussels non-profit sector. The scheme works through a dual lever: a **regional premium** (indexed to civil service pay scales) and an **exemption from basic employer social security contributions** (target-group reduction of the ONSS/RSZ). Positions are intended for jobseekers registered with Actiris, with eligibility conditions linked to unemployment duration, age, or disability.

By end 2017, approximately **10,000 positions** were subsidised across **1,312 employers**: over 7,000 in non-profits and administrations ("Programme Law" positions) and approximately 2,500 in local authorities. At the end of 2021, the "Loi-programme" scheme, excluding local authorities, employed **6,906 workers**, or 5,402 full-time equivalents, according to view.brussels; the Employment Minister referred to **5,500 workers** in September 2026. Much of the fall since 2017 stems from the transfer of municipal and CPAS posts to Brussels Local Authorities in 2021 (see the section of 18-21 September 2026).

## What are the conditions for an ACS contract?

They are set by the Order of the Government of the Brussels-Capital Region of 28 November 2002 on the subsidised contract worker scheme. Its last recorded amendment dates from 2 May 2024.

**For the worker**, you must:

- be registered with **Actiris** as an unemployed jobseeker;
- have been registered as an unemployed jobseeker for **at least six months** during the year before recruitment, or have received the **social integration income** or social assistance for six months over the same period;
- hold the **diploma, certificate or qualification** required for the post, or the corresponding professional experience.

The six months are not required, among others, from fully unemployed benefit recipients and social integration income recipients aged **40** or over, from people receiving a disability allowance, or from people hired by a school or a childcare provider.

On **residence**, Actiris requires in its pages and ACS job offers that candidates live in the Brussels Region. The 2002 Order only provides for **priority**: Actiris first proposes candidates who live in the Region, then those registered for the longest time. An **eligibility certificate**, available in My Actiris, is required for the interview and for signing the contract.

**For the employer**, ACS workers can be hired by non-profit associations (excluding hospitals), regional and community administrations and those of the community commissions, schools and public housing companies. The activity must be non-commercial and carried out in the Brussels Region. The employer signs an **agreement with Actiris** after approval by the Brussels ministers for Employment and the Budget, and then has six months to recruit. It receives a **regional premium** and is exempted from basic employer social security contributions. New posts remain possible in targeted programmes: the 2021-2026 early childhood protocol funds one full-time-equivalent ACS post for every seven childcare places created. In municipalities and CPAS, the ACS status was largely abolished on 1 January 2021; it remains for the Cigogne plan, the VGC, integration contracts and former FBI posts.

**Reform**: the Regional Policy Declaration of 13 February 2026 provides for monitoring and an evaluation of the ACS system. Associations had until 13 September 2026 to complete an Actiris questionnaire sent on 16 July. According to CESSoC, a federation of socio-cultural employers, no concrete information on a reform was available as of 5 August 2026. On 21 September 2026, the Employment Minister said the questionnaire had closed with a 92% response rate and that there was no question of reducing ACS jobs (see the section of 18-21 September 2026).

## History and competence transfer

The ACS scheme has existed since **1987**, originally created to combat Brussels unemployment. The **6th State Reform** (2014) transferred employment competence — including ACS — from the federal to the regional level, giving the Brussels-Capital Region full responsibility.

Unlike Flanders (which transformed APE/ACS into a "Vlaamse Ondersteuningspremie" system) and Wallonia (APE reform completed in 2022), **Brussels has not yet completed its reform**. The scheme has since operated under a transitional regime.

## The 2015 reform: shift towards activation

In **March 2015**, the Brussels government launched a major reform through Actiris. Key measures:

- **Elimination of 100% ministerial exemptions** for subsidies
- **6-month deadline** to fill a vacant position, otherwise the position is lost (the Order allows Actiris to extend it by six months at most)
- **Elimination of advance payments** for new hires after 1 March 2015
- **Systematic evaluation** of all positions: 351 non-profits classified as "high risk"
- **Restriction of diploma derogations**: upward derogations (allowing overqualified candidates) progressively abolished to refocus on low-skilled target groups

This shift marks a **philosophical change**: ACS moves from structural support for the social sector to an **activation policy for the unemployed**, where the position should serve as a stepping stone to sustainable employment. The non-profit sector criticises this shift: ACS positions often fulfil permanent and essential functions (home care, childcare, social cohesion) incompatible with a "stepping stone" logic.

## The ACS differential: structural underfunding

The core problem identified by BRUXEO (non-profit employers' federation) is the **"ACS differential"**: employers must offer ACS workers the same salaries and conditions as regular staff, but the regional premium does not cover the full cost.

Three factors widen this gap:

1. **Non-profit agreements** (COCOF/COCOM) provide higher salary scales than those recognised by the ACS premium
2. The premium only accounts for **ACS seniority**, not prior sector experience
3. **New benefits** (additional leave days, bonuses) apply to ACS workers **without supplementary budget**

Employers therefore absorb the difference, which weakens the smallest and most subsidy-dependent organisations.

## Non-profit mobilisation (February 2026)

On **3 February 2026**, hundreds of non-profit sector workers demonstrated outside 16 rue de la Loi in Brussels, demanding a **sectoral social agreement** and immediate investment. The unions (CNE, CGSLB, FGTB) denounce:

- The deterioration of working conditions in essential sectors (care, social work, childcare)
- The freeze on subsidised employment (according to BRUXEO, no new ACS position has been created since 2015, early childhood aside)
- The announced postponement of a social agreement to **2028**, deemed unacceptable

A **national demonstration** is planned for **12 March 2026** in Brussels (gathering at Gare du Nord, 10 AM). Organised by the joint union front (FGTB, CSC, CGSLB), it extends beyond the non-profit sector: demands cover pensions ("Jambon malus"), unemployment/sickness sanctions, the "light" index skip, and disinvestment in public services. A strike notice covers SNCB (8-11 March) and STIB (12 March).

## Elimination of ACS insertion positions (December 2023)

In December 2023, the outgoing Brussels government eliminated the **550 ACS insertion positions** — a programme from the previous legislature targeting young people under 26 with low qualifications in the non-profit sector. Minister Clerfayt's office cited an evaluation by the Federal Planning Bureau and IBSA concluding that the programme was not meeting its objectives. The sector denounced the lack of prior consultation and the abrupt termination without transitional measures.

## Compensation subsidy for local authorities

The ACS reform in communes and CPAS was compensated by an **annual regional subsidy**. The decree of 5 December 2024 sets this subsidy at **EUR 43.8 million** for fiscal year 2024, covering communes, CPAS and Les Cuisines bruxelloises. The amount is calculated based on 2018 reference costs, indexed at 2% per year for the social security contribution share. In return, employers must systematically transmit all job vacancies to Actiris. The Order of 20 November 2025 sets the subsidy at **EUR 44.16 million** for fiscal year 2025. ACS status remains there for the Cigogne plan, the VGC, integration contracts and former FBI posts.

## RPD 2026: what prospects?

The Regional Policy Declaration of 13 February 2026 provides that the Government will carry out **monitoring and an evaluation of the ACS system** to better match it to the needs of the various sectors and organisations. The dossier is linked to other employment commitments (70% employment rate target, enhanced activation, bilingualism). The split of Employment/Training competences between two different ministers at COCOF (Hublet for Employment, Dilliès for Training) complicates coordination.

## Actiris 2026 budget adjusted (April 2026)

On **26 March 2026**, the Actiris Management Committee rejected an initial version of the 2026 budget during a meeting described as "extremely tense": employer representatives (Beci, UCM) and social partners (unions, non-profit sector) could not agree on the **€40 million savings** imposed on employment policies by the new Brussels government. Director-General **Cristina Amboldi**, feeling unsupported, walked out of the meeting and then submitted her resignation, **announced on Monday 30 March 2026** by the Employment Minister's office. Her deputy Caroline Mancel ended her mandate on 31 March. The Management Committee handles day-to-day operations during the recruitment of new leadership.

On **Thursday 16 April 2026**, the Brussels government reached an agreement on an adjusted Actiris 2026 budget. According to Employment Minister **Laurent Hublet** (Les Engagés), the initial €40M savings effort is reduced to **~€28M** in 2026 (−€12M). Main concessions regarding ACS and the non-profit sector:

- **Article 20 preserved**: no suppression of the derogation mechanism
- **95% harmonisation dropped**: differentiated subsidy rates maintained
- **Actiris partnerships policy**: integral ambitions maintained, **€67M** preserved
- **Consolidated ACS budget**: **€276M** in 2026
- **First Employment Agreements (CPE)**: significant share maintained in Brussels administrations

The **Activa.brussels** premium is confirmed abolished, replaced by a new scheme in 2027. According to **Florence Lepoivre** (FGTB Brussels), an unspecified study reportedly showed these premiums did not mainly benefit Brussels residents — a claim not echoed by the government and not found in the available institutional evaluation (IDEA Consult, March 2023), which instead documents a low windfall effect for this scheme. Lepoivre nonetheless describes the Activa reform as "perhaps the least problematic measure", while recalling broader concerns about regional budget constraints. On 3 July 2026, the Brussels Parliament formally voted the abolition: see the dedicated [Activa.brussels dossier](/en/dossiers/activa) for the official rationale and full timeline.

The adjusted budget still needs to be presented to the Actiris Management Committee and translated into a regional budget adjustment voted by the Brussels Parliament.

## ACS monitoring: an opinion piece by 200 associations, and the minister's reply (18-21 September 2026)

On Friday 18 September 2026, Le Soir published an opinion piece entitled "Défendons les emplois ACS et le tissu associatif bruxellois" (Let us defend ACS jobs and the Brussels voluntary sector), led by the Plateforme ACS. It is signed by more than **200 Brussels associations and organisations**, by the CNE, SETCa-FGTB, FGTB Bruxelles and CSC Bruxelles trade unions, and by 157 workers in their own name. The signatories put the scheme at 5,600 jobs. They are concerned about the questionnaire sent to associations in July, whose purposes and treatment of the answers collected remain, in their view, unclear, and fear that it is preparing a refocusing of the scheme or its reorientation towards the activation of jobseekers. They call on the Brussels government to renounce any reform of ACS, while stating that they do not contest the principle of monitoring.

On Monday 21 September 2026, questioned in the plenary session of the Brussels Parliament by MP Farida Tahar (Ecolo), the Brussels Employment Minister, **Laurent Hublet** (Les Engagés), said: "Je souhaite rassurer pleinement le secteur : Il n'est pas question de réduire les emplois ACS." (I want to fully reassure the sector: there is no question of reducing ACS jobs.) According to him, the scheme represents a budget of **276 million euros** and supports more than **1,000 associations** and **5,500 workers**. He said the questionnaire sent by Actiris closed on 13 September 2026 with a response rate of 92%, and that the data will be cross-checked with those held by Actiris before consultation with the non-profit sector. The evaluation, he added, does not call into question the usefulness of the scheme.

The question is listed in the Parliament's database (topical question no. 13, answered on 21 September 2026). The record of the sitting had not yet been published as of 24 September 2026: the minister's words are quoted from the Belga news agency dispatch.

### How many ACS jobs? Figures that do not count the same thing

The published orders of magnitude vary by a factor of two because they cover neither the same date nor the same scope.

| Figure | Date | What it counts | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| around 10,000 posts | end of 2017 | subsidised posts, including around 2,500 in municipalities and CPAS (2,537 people according to view.brussels) | BRUXEO |
| 6,906 workers, or 5,402 full-time equivalents | 31 December 2021 | "Loi-programme" scheme (non-profits, administrations, schools), excluding local authorities | view.brussels |
| 5,600 jobs | September 2026 | scope not specified | associations' opinion piece |
| 5,500 workers, more than 1,000 associations | September 2026 | scope not specified | Employment Minister |

The fall between 2017 and 2021 is largely explained by the ACS reform in local authorities: since 1 January 2021, 85% of these posts have been transferred, for management and budget, to Brussels Local Authorities (Bruxelles Pouvoirs locaux), and only 10% are still managed by Actiris, according to view.brussels. The gap between the 6,906 workers of 2021 and the 5,500 cited in 2026 is, however, explained by no published source: neither the minister nor the opinion piece specifies whether these are people or full-time equivalents, or whether administrations and schools are included.

## Issues to watch

- **Non-profit social agreement**: will an agreement be reached before 2028, as unions demand?
- **ACS differential**: will the Region increase premiums to bridge the salary gap?
- **Position creation**: will the freeze in place since 2015 be lifted to meet growing needs (home care, childcare, social cohesion)?
- **Employment/Training coordination**: will the COCOF split between Hublet and Dilliès allow coherent management of Actiris and Bruxelles Formation?
- **Unemployment reform impact**: according to ONEM, 26,986 Brussels residents reached the end of their benefit entitlement between January and June 2026, and a majority of them remain registered with Actiris; the pressure is shifting onto the CPAS and the non-profit sector (see the [Brussels CPAS](/en/dossiers/cpas-bruxellois) dossier).
- **ACS monitoring**: what results will be drawn from the questionnaire closed on 13 September 2026, and on what consultation timetable? The minister gave no date in his reply of 21 September, as reported by Belga.
