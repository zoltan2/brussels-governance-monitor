## Overview

In 2025, **2,208 businesses** were declared bankrupt in the Brussels-Capital Region, a **13.2%** increase compared to 2024. This is the strongest regional increase in Belgium: Flanders recorded +7.8%, while Wallonia saw a decrease of -3.0%.

At the national level, **11,837 bankruptcies** were pronounced in 2025, up 5.9%. The bankruptcy rate relative to the number of active businesses (1,575,898) stands at **0.75%**.

For 2025, the FPS Economy recorded **11,679 SMEs** going bankrupt in Belgium (businesses with fewer than 250 employees), resulting in the loss of **26,747 jobs**, an average of 2.3 jobs lost per bankruptcy. Statbel counts, for all businesses in the Brussels-Capital Region in 2025 combined (all sizes), **2,190 bankruptcies** and **6,003 jobs lost**.

**Methodological note**: the Brussels enterprise court sometimes pronounces judicial dissolutions rather than bankruptcies in the strict sense. Official Statbel statistics only count bankruptcies, which may underestimate the actual number of business closures. The GraydonCreditsafe figures (2,208 bankruptcies in 2025, provisional as of 30 December 2025) and Statbel figures (2,190, final figure) differ slightly due to distinct methodologies.

**First half of 2026: new record.** According to Graydon, **6,267 businesses** were declared bankrupt in Belgium in the first half of 2026, a **4%** increase and a historic record. Flanders accounts for most of the rise (3,684 bankruptcies, also a record), while Wallonia declined slightly (1,341, -1%). For Brussels, Statbel counts **1,098 bankruptcies** in the first half of 2026 (+2.7% compared with 2025), a figure close to the one put forward by Graydon via 21News (around 1,092, +3.8%). Statbel also confirms the national record: 6,260 bankruptcies at the Belgian level over the same period, the highest in its series since 2005.

**First eight months of 2026: stability at a high level.** According to Statbel, 1,299 businesses were declared bankrupt in the Brussels-Capital Region from January to August 2026, against 1,306 in the same period of 2025 (-0.5%). July was busier than a year earlier (123 against 110), August markedly less so (78 against 127). Jobs lost fell more sharply: 3,351 against 4,074 (-17.7%). The January-August 2026 total remains below the levels of 2018 (1,900) and 2019 (1,966): this is not a Brussels record, unlike the national level.

## Hardest-hit sectors

### Construction

The construction sector is among the most affected. In 2024, **405 construction businesses** went bankrupt in Brussels. At the national level, the first half of 2025 saw **1,499 bankruptcies** in the construction sector, according to Statbel. From January to August 2026, construction recorded **209 bankruptcies** in Brussels (-16.1% year-on-year), accounting for 300 jobs lost. The suspension of Renolution grants directly weakened Brussels construction businesses.

### Hospitality (horeca)

In Brussels, hospitality accounted for **16.9%** of bankruptcies in 2025 and **18.1%** from January to August 2026, according to Statbel, or 235 bankruptcies over the first eight months of the year (+4.4% year-on-year), accounting for 633 jobs lost. The lifespan of business concepts has shrunk from a historical 7-10 years to 3-5 years currently. The hotel VAT increase from 6% to 12% on 1 March 2026 adds further pressure.

### Retail

Retail is affected by the rise in vacant commercial premises: a **13.5%** vacancy rate in Brussels (approximately 3,500 vacant units, according to hub.brussels). From January to August 2026, the sector recorded **280 bankruptcies** in Brussels (-9.1% year-on-year), according to Statbel, accounting for 564 jobs lost.

## Factors cited by official actors

Institutional sources identify several converging factors:

- **Debts accumulated during COVID**, not yet absorbed (enterprise court)
- **Inflation and high energy costs** (FPS Economy)
- **Pressure on mature businesses** (5-10 years old), established just before successive crises (GraydonCreditsafe)
- **Suspension of Renolution grants** — direct impact on Brussels construction (Embuild Brussels)
- **Hotel VAT increase** 6% → 12% on 1 March 2026

BGM lists these factors as cited by official sources, without ranking them or providing causal analysis.

## Notable case: GH Group / Gérald Hibert (April 2026)

On **20 April 2026**, the Brussels enterprise court granted **GH Group** (Brussels real estate developer Gérald Hibert) an **additional one-month grace period** as part of judicial reorganisation proceedings. The case has been adjourned to **18 May 2026**. The group's **22 companies** were placed under **provisional administration** at the end of March 2026.

**Figures cited in the press** (source: BRUZZ Stedenbouw, 20 April):

- Estimated real estate portfolio: **€1 to €1.5 billion** — including the Apple, Delvaux and Armani buildings on Avenue de la Toison d'Or / Louise
- Debts owed to Belgian banks: ~**€300 million**
- Tax + social security debts: ~**€30 million**
- Announced recapitalisation offer: US investment fund **Revetas** is reported ready to inject **€500 million** (part capital, part credits) — amount and identity to be confirmed by primary source (court or parties)

The Hibert case illustrates the Brussels real estate market's exposure to mid-to-large-size bankruptcies — a segment poorly documented in the consolidated Statbel / GraydonCreditsafe statistics, which count by number of entities without measuring economic or patrimonial footprint.

**Outcome (25-30 June 2026).** On **25 June 2026**, **21 of the 22 companies** in the GH Group acknowledged the conditions for bankruptcy before the Brussels enterprise court. For the last entity, **Sogefibel**, the provisional administrator withdrew the bankruptcy request on **30 June** in favour of **judicial reorganisation (PRJ)** proceedings; the creditor banks (ING, BNP Paribas Fortis, KBC) indicated they reserve the right to seek bankruptcy again for this company if the PRJ does not succeed.

**Follow-up (July-September 2026).** Sogefibel, the group's last entity, has been in judicial reorganisation proceedings since the opening hearing on **8 July 2026**, under reorganisation practitioner Roman Aydogdu, assisted by Pierre Hermant (CEO of finance&invest.brussels), with an initial four-month stay. In early September, Gérald Hibert's counsel filed an appeal against the bankruptcy ruling for the other 21 companies, arguing that there had been no bankruptcy acknowledgement (La Libre, 3 September 2026). According to the receivers cited by La Libre, no assets had been sold at that point.

## Regional mechanisms

The Brussels-Capital Region has several mechanisms to support businesses in difficulty:

| Mechanism | Role | Operator |
|-----------|------|----------|
| Centre pour Entreprises en difficulté (CEd Relance) | Free assessment, referral to mediation or a PRJ lawyer | BECI (with regional support) |
| PRJ / mediation | Partial coverage of judicial reorganisation costs | Region |
| hub.brussels / 1819 / hub.info | Information, referral, credit mediation | hub.brussels |
| Revival by Pulse Foundation | Post-bankruptcy support (coaching, mentoring) | Pulse Foundation |
| Un pass dans l'impasse | 4 free psychological support sessions | — |
| Oasis (Réseau Entreprendre Bruxelles) | Assessment and 3-month support programme | Réseau Entreprendre |
| Reload Yourself (JobYourself) | Support for resuming activity, up to 24 months | JobYourself |

**Open questions**: the number of cases handled by the CEd Relance relative to the total number of bankruptcies, and the capacity of these mechanisms in the face of the rise in bankruptcies since 2024, are not publicly documented at this stage. The chamber for businesses in difficulty of the French-speaking enterprise court, not to be confused with BECI's CEd Relance despite the similar acronym, handled **2,315 cases** in 2025, against 3,526 in 2024, according to its annual report. The CEd Relance for its part has an annual budget of 320,000 euros, according to BRUZZ (28 April 2026).

## Courts and judicial reorganisation

The French-speaking Brussels enterprise court pronounced 1,743 bankruptcies in 2025 (around +11%), according to its annual report relayed by BX1. By the end of April 2026, it had already received more than 100 requests for judicial reorganisation according to its president, against 172 over the whole of 2025 and 148 in 2024. These figures cover only the French-speaking court and cannot be added to the regional Statbel count, which follows a different methodology.

According to L'Echo, relayed by BRUZZ, the regional system of subsidised mediation for businesses in difficulty has been at a standstill since 2024, for lack of a government; BECI is asking the Region for 650,000 euros a year to relaunch it. The Secretary of State for the Economy, Barbara Trachte, for her part said in committee on 4 February 2026 that she wanted to make regional support for judicial reorganisation permanent. The current status of this support is not publicly confirmed.

## Coverage in the Regional Policy Declaration

The Regional Policy Declaration of 13 February 2026 does not explicitly mention bankruptcies or the CEd. The economic measures identified concern:

- **Urban Free Zone** (Port of Brussels + Audi Forest site) — tax relief, single administrative window, dedicated Actiris unit
- **hub.brussels** — continuation of international economic missions
- **Innoviris** — integration with Finance.Brussels

The Regional Policy Declaration's approach is oriented towards attractiveness (attracting new investment) rather than supporting existing businesses in difficulty.

On **22 June 2026**, the Brussels Minister for the Economy and Employment, Laurent Hublet, presented his roadmap "Brussels All.In", which includes an SME support plan called "Oxygen", still under study, as well as the strengthening of Brusoc (finance&invest.brussels). In his 2026 budget statement before the Brussels Parliament (doc [A-268/2](http://weblex.brussels/data/crb/doc/2025-26/153895/images.pdf), 23 March 2026), the minister cited bankruptcies among the Region's foremost economic challenges and announced an economic redeployment plan as well as a transformation of hub.brussels. This roadmap is a ministerial document distinct from the Regional Policy Declaration itself, which remains unchanged since 13 February 2026.

## What the data does not tell us

Bankruptcy statistics do not capture voluntary closures, judicial dissolutions, or businesses in difficulty that have not yet reached the bankruptcy threshold. The actual number of businesses in distress is likely higher than official figures.

The available data also does not allow measuring the cascading impact: indirect job losses, effects on suppliers, transformation of commercial districts, or the psychological impact on entrepreneurs.
