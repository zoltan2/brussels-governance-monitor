## Audi site in Forest: the takeover agreement with Heylen Warehouses is concluded (1 September 2026)

On **Tuesday 1 September 2026**, the **Heylen Warehouses** group, **Audi** and the **Brussels-Capital Region** announced an agreement on the takeover of the Audi Brussels site in Forest. The project carries the provisional name **Rings Park 21**.

**This is not the discovery of a buyer.** Heylen Warehouses had already been selected in **March 2026**, as the Forest commune card documents. The fact of 1 September is the conclusion of the agreement, and the parties state that the legal documents are still being finalised.

What the agreement provides for:

- a mixed urban business park of around **55 hectares**, the largest industrial site in the Region;
- a target of **at least three thousand jobs**;
- the retention and development of industrial activities, space for SMEs and innovative companies, partial public access to the site, and an upgrade of Forest-Midi station;
- first activity **from early 2027**, initially covering a few hundred jobs.

Two timeframes coexist in the statements, and we report them as they stand: the regional minister for the Economy and Employment speaks of **three to five years** to reach three thousand jobs, while the buyer refers to around **ten years** to roll out the whole project.

### The figure that gives the measure

Audi Brussels employed around **three thousand people** before closing on **28 February 2025**. The announced target therefore matches the number of jobs lost, but spread over several years and in different activities: logistics and handling in the southern part of the site, productive activities requiring skilled workers elsewhere.

### What this card does not write

The **takeover amount has not been disclosed**. An order of magnitude of around one hundred million euros circulates in a single press source, which itself presents it as an estimate. BGM does not publish it as a fact.

On the regional side, the agreement comes with an **economic acceleration zone** and a **dedicated training unit at Actiris**, with financial support presented as conditional on meeting the job-creation commitments. The detail of those conditions is not public.

**Reservation on the surface area.** The Forest commune card has stated **54 hectares** since March 2026, whereas the sources of 1 September refer to around 55. No source explains the gap; we retain "around 55 hectares" and flag the variation.

## Brussels casino: the Council of State suspends the award (31 August 2026)

## Municipal office tax: City of Brussels ruled in violation of the equality principle (22 June 2026)

The Dutch-speaking court of first instance of Brussels ruled, on **22 June 2026**, that the **City of Brussels'** tax regulation on office space (assessment years 2022 to 2025) violates the **constitutional principle of equality**, for lack of objective justification to distinguish office space from space used for other purposes. ## Several business support schemes suspended from 12 August 2026

Announced on **31 July 2026** and effective **Wednesday 12 August 2026**, the suspension covers a set of regional business support schemes and applies, according to the regional communication, for the remainder of the 2026 budget year.

**Schemes suspended**: consultancy aid, economic transition aid, external training, recruitment aid and the coworking premium, which could reach 1,500 euros. BX1 also mentions consultancy linked to business transfer or takeover. The count varies across newsrooms, from four to six schemes, depending on the scope used.

**The reason is credits running out mid-year.** The annual envelope covering all consultancy and investment aid amounts to **30 million euros**. On **13 July 2026**, only **1.86 million euros** remained, around **6 %**, while investment aid already showed a 4.75 million euro shortfall. The volume of pending files is reported differently: around **2,200 applications** expected by year end according to BX1, **2,881 applications** already received according to BRUZZ, with a risk of 400 applications going unfunded by year end if nothing were done.

**What this reveals.** This is not a scheme abolished by prior decision, but a counter closed for lack of credits, decided mid-year and notified to businesses twelve days before it took effect. The sectors cited as most exposed are hospitality, small structures and service providers, for whom these schemes often cover start-up or transition support. According to Trends-Tendances, the relevant cabinet says the decision had been settled since June.

This episode adds to the cuts already documented on this card concerning the hub.brussels international network and illustrates the tension between the stated goal of economic attractiveness and actual budget execution.
