## 2024 accounts: the Council of State rejects the municipality's request for suspension (23 November 2026)

In its ruling no. 268.442 of Monday 23 November 2026, the Council of State, sitting in summary proceedings with three members, rejected the request for suspension filed on 30 September 2026 by Saint-Josse-ten-Noode against the decree of the Brussels minister for local authorities of 30 July 2026. That decree refused to approve the second version of the 2024 accounts, voted by the municipal council on 25 March 2026, after a first refusal on 21 January 2026. The first auditor had given a concurring opinion. The action for annulment is still pending and costs are reserved.

The refusal rested on two grounds: the annex of open receivables does not match the balance sheet (16.13 million euros of short-term receivables in subsidies on the balance sheet, 10.51 million in the table of open receivables), and thirteen individual accounts show balances that must be checked and justified. The Council of State finds none of the municipality's pleas serious. It considers that the supervisor could not correct these entries itself, for example an account of "miscellaneous unidentified items" of 906,015.87 euros which, according to the administrative file, nobody at the municipality can trace. In this summary examination, it dismisses the claims of discriminatory treatment and misuse of power: the minister departed from the recommendation of his administration, which proposed in a note of 10 July 2026 to let the supervision deadline expire, but the municipality's bookkeeping has been problematic for several years, and the municipality brings no concrete evidence of a political aim. The ruling settles neither urgency nor the municipality's standing, which the Region had disputed since the vote on a third version of the accounts.

The ruling reproduces the minister's second warning, sent by a letter dated 2 October 2026: according to that letter, the municipality has accumulated 1,394 days of delay in submitting its accounts over the 2019-2024 period, and 1,318 days for its 2019-2025 budgets; the 2026 budget had not been submitted. The ruling also quotes the Brussels government's decision of 28 May 2026, which instructs the minister for local authorities to submit to it in December 2026 the appointment of a commissioner if the shortcomings persist. To meet its cash needs, the municipality obtained two short-term loans, for 21 million euros, from the Brussels Regional Fund for the Refinancing of Municipal Treasuries (FRBRTC).

Before the Council of State, the municipality stated that the third version of the 2024 accounts, voted on 28 October 2026, had not been sent to the supervisor and that it did not intend to send it before the ruling. According to La Libre of 24 November 2026, that transmission had been postponed until the ruling. In a press release of 24 November 2026 carried by Belga and quoted by BRUZZ, the municipality says it has sent that version to the supervisor and wants it approved quickly so that it can adopt the 2026 budget; it gives no date of transmission.

## Municipal treasurer: the nationality requirement of the recruitment questioned (November 2026)

According to La DH of Tuesday 17 November 2026, the municipality opened in summer 2026, at the request of the regional supervisory authority, the recruitment of a municipal treasurer, a post held for four years by an acting treasurer appointed without an examination. The recruitment rules and the letter sent to staff required Belgian nationality. Of five candidates, the outgoing treasurer was the only one to pass the examinations; he was appointed but remains acting, for lack of a sufficient level of Dutch, and has two years to reach it. He is not Belgian. An Ecolo municipal councillor has alerted the regional supervisory authority.

According to Brulocalis, the association of Brussels cities and municipalities, consulted by La DH, the New Municipal Law reserves posts that involve the exercise of public authority, including that of treasurer, to nationals of Belgium, the European Union or the European Economic Area: an EU national can therefore be treasurer, which is the case in Saint-Josse. La DH notes that other municipalities have also required Belgian nationality for this type of recruitment. The municipality says it is examining the question with the regional supervisory authority. The treasurer is one of the three shortcomings the supervisory authority wants resolved (section of 28 October 2026 below).

## Georges Pètre tennis hall: supervision suspends a handover, the municipality prepares a call for candidates (November 2026)

According to L'Avenir of Monday 16 November 2026, the tennis hall on the Georges Pètre site, owned by Saint-Josse-ten-Noode but located on the territory of Evere, stands empty and is being damaged (break-ins, thefts). In 2006 the municipality entrusted it to a company under a thirty-year agreement, for a fee of EUR 3,000 a month. In the early 2010s, the operator, which accused the municipality of poor maintenance, stopped paying the fee. An agreement was reached in 2024: the municipality waived the arrears, EUR 290,000 according to the mayor (EUR 365,000 according to the figure presented to the municipal council, which the mayor says was out of date), and paid the operator EUR 160,000 in compensation by ending the agreement on grounds of public interest, namely the overall renovation of the site. According to the paper, that EUR 15 million renovation project, including EUR 10 million in regional subsidies, did not go ahead because the Region did not commit to it.

Approached in June 2026 by a company offering to restore the hall and then run it, the municipality made the site available to that company. This decision was suspended by regional supervision, which asked in particular for justification of the award method. Rather than justify its choice before the municipal council in July 2026, the municipality decided to launch a call for candidates. According to the mayor, quoted by L'Avenir on 16 November 2026, the college was to approve the selection criteria the following week; the partner chosen will have to restore the hall and offer sports activities there, and the company that had come forward earlier (a five-a-side football and padel project) stands a good chance, in his view. In the opposition, an Ecolo councillor argues that the municipality could have ended the agreement for the operator's failures, without paying compensation.

## Coercive supervision: the municipal council authorises three Council of State appeals, 2025 accounts approved (12 November 2026)

According to a Belga news agency report carried by L'Avenir, Le Soir, La DH and BRUZZ, the municipal council of Saint-Josse-ten-Noode authorised the college on Thursday 12 November 2026, majority against opposition, to bring three appeals before the Council of State against the Region's coercive supervision procedure. They target the Brussels government's decision of 28 May 2026 to start the procedure and the warnings of 5 June and 2 October 2026, according to the report (the government had announced the second warning on 24 September 2026). The municipality contests the proportionality of the measure, which it regards as an infringement of its autonomy, and intends to claim compensation for the harm it says it has suffered; the mayor gave no amount. In the opposition, a PS councillor puts lawyers' and procedural costs for 2024 and 2025 at nearly EUR 400,000, a figure the mayor disputes, and an Ecolo councillor questions the admissibility of the appeals and the legal deadlines.

The same evening the council approved the accounts for 2025, majority against the PS and Ecolo opposition. They show a cumulative deficit of nearly EUR 25 million. According to the same report, the mayor explains that this deficit includes EUR 7.4 million of uncollectable receivables in 2025, after EUR 6.9 million in 2024, and puts the 2025 deficit on current operations at EUR 4.8 million. The accounts still have to be sent to the regional supervisory authority, which has rejected the 2024 accounts twice; the 2026 budget remains blocked pending validation of the 2024 and 2025 accounts.

## Coercive supervision: the municipality prepares three Council of State appeals (7 November 2026)

According to La DH of Saturday 7 November 2026, the Brussels government replied late and unfavourably to the formal notice of 15 October, which asked it to halt the coercive supervision procedure. The procedure could be completed in December 2026, according to the paper. The municipality is considering **three appeals to the Council of State**: one against each of the two warnings and one against the decision to launch the procedure. The municipal council authorised the college to lodge them on **Thursday 12 November 2026** (see the next section).

These appeals come on top of the one the municipality maintains against the annulment of its 2024 accounts, despite the approval of a new version on 28 October. The PS group leader, quoted by La DH, states that the Council of State's auditor proposes rejecting that appeal, and that the municipality spent 389,000 euros on lawyers' and procedural fees in 2024 and 2025. The Ecolo group also criticises the string of appeals. The Council of State's ruling of 23 November 2026 confirms that the first auditor gave a concurring opinion on the rejection of the request for suspension (section of 23 November 2026 above); no other source confirms the legal fees put forward by the opposition.

## 2024 accounts: the municipal council approves the third version, 2025 accounts postponed (28 October 2026)

The municipal council meeting of Wednesday 28 October 2026 approved the third version of the 2024 accounts (BX1, BRUZZ). According to BX1, the PS and Ecolo groups voted against. The vote on the 2025 accounts was postponed after the opposition pointed out that the documents submitted to the council were not compliant.

According to BX1, this version shows a deficit of nearly 12 million euros, against 11.2 million in the previous version, a difference of about 700,000 euros. Regional supervision, which had rejected the two previous versions, has yet to rule. As long as the 2024 accounts are not approved, the municipality cannot submit its 2026 budget (BRUZZ). ## Belgium's densest municipality

Saint-Josse-ten-Noode is the smallest (1.16 km²) and densest municipality in Belgium (~23,185 inhabitants/km²). After the **annulment** of the October 2024 elections due to irregularities (proxy voting), a new vote was held on 9 February 2025. Emir Kir (independent, ex-PS) reinforced his absolute majority to 18/29 seats (55.6%).

## Transparency: 3 out of 6 criteria

The **budget** is published online, the council has been broadcast via **livestream** on YouTube since 2018, and the **mandate registry** is accessible. Council **minutes** are partial (publication delays reported), **citizen participation** is limited to advisory councils, and there is no **Open Data** portal.

## Financial crisis and coercive supervision

Saint-Josse is going through a **financial crisis**. The regional government reports a **cumulative deficit of over EUR 30 million** (2025 financial year), an amount **contested by the mayor**, who puts it at around **EUR 17 million**, points to "twenty-two million already saved" and states he has "not asked the Region for a single euro in twenty-four years". Between 2025 and 2026 the **Brussels Regional Fund for the Refinancing of Municipal Treasuries (FRBRTC)** intervened **three times, for EUR 21 million** in total, according to L'Avenir of 28 May 2026; the Council of State's ruling of 23 November 2026 refers to **two short-term loans**, for EUR 21 million, and the sources do not say how the two counts relate; the municipality requested a deferral to repay a **EUR 7 million loan**.

On **28 May 2026** the regional **cabinet** **activated the coercive supervision procedure** (tutelle coercitive) over the municipality, on the initiative of the **minister for local authorities**, citing "shortcomings and unlawful practices" and "systemic dysfunctions" identified by the Brussels Local Authorities administration. The procedure provides for formal warnings and, if the irregularities persist, the appointment of a **special commissioner** tasked with restoring the finances and overseeing municipal decisions. This is the **first municipal coercive supervision in Brussels since the Schaerbeek counters affair (1976)**.

The first formal warning provided for by the procedure was sent to the municipality on **5 June 2026**. On **24 September 2026**, the government examined the municipality's situation again, and the minister for local authorities announced a **second warning**, with a deadline of at most three months: see the section "Coercive supervision: second warning to the municipality (24 September 2026)".

To restore its finances, the municipality had already taken drastic measures: a massive increase in **property tax** (PRI) from 2,980 to **4,090 additional centimes** (the steepest rise in the Region) and the abolition of the municipal staff **year-end bonus**. A **fatal shooting** in January 2026 reignited security concerns.

## North Quarter: Council of State auditors reject the emergency appeals (10 August 2026)

On **Monday 10 August 2026**, the **Council of State** heard two appeals against the compulsory night-time closure of shops in the North Quarter, between **1 a.m. and 6 a.m.**:

- an **emergency suspension appeal** brought by **Schaerbeek shopkeepers**;
- an appeal against the same measure on the **Saint-Josse** side, brought by a **sex worker** and by the organisations **Espace P** and **Utsopi**.

Both applications alleged shortcomings in the measure's justification, particularly on **proportionality**.

### What the auditors' reports say

The auditors **reject the urgency** in both cases, finding it insufficiently established. In the Schaerbeek file, the auditor **also ruled on the merits**, on an extremely urgent basis, in the municipality's favour. No analysis on the merits was issued for the Saint-Josse file.

Both municipalities therefore get a first favourable signal, but **with reservations**: according to the press, the report is markedly less clear-cut on the merits than on urgency, and leaves doubt about a possible **extension** beyond the end of September 2026.

### Why the distinction matters

An **auditor's report is not a judgment**. The auditor investigates and proposes; the court decides, and may depart from it. At this stage no judicial decision has been handed down on the measure's legality.

**Timeline reminder**: the night-time closure was adopted by **Schaerbeek** and **Saint-Josse-ten-Noode** in March 2026, took effect in early April, and was **extended in June to the end of September 2026**. Schaerbeek relaxed the hour to **3 a.m.** on Friday-to-Saturday and Saturday-to-Sunday nights and on the eve of public holidays; Saint-Josse did not follow that relaxation.
