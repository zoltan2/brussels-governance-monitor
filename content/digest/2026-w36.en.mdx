## Week of 31 August to 6 September 2026

### SLRB Funding Crisis

On **3 September 2026**, the MR blocked the **131 million euros** in regional funding allocated to the SLRB for 2026, the holding company supervising sixteen public housing corporations. The expected transfer, due over the summer, did not arrive. This explicit political move, conditional on the resignation of the president of Foyer anderlechtois, re-elected on 17 August, threatens the SLRB's ability to meet obligations from September onward and would force additional asset sales. Context: **62,234 households** are waiting for social housing in Brussels, and the February 2026 agreement provided for 400 million euros and over one thousand additional public units.

### LEZ: First Numbers on Enforcement Phase

The enforcement phase of the Low Emission Zone, active since **1 July 2026**, generated **25,905 warnings** in the first half year, **1,890 fines** since July, and approximately **255 fines actually paid** (thus **13% of total**). Revenue stands at **89,152.50 EUR**. The target area covers approximately **29,000 Brussels vehicles** and **630,000** nationally. These figures must be weighed against network degradation: **23% of regional ANPR cameras** are out of service, and only **48% of flash images** are usable by Brussels Fiscalité.

### Climate: Over Two Hundred Excess Deaths

Sciensano published on **2 September 2026** the consolidated assessment of three summer heat waves. Brussels records **over two hundred excess deaths**: **196** for the June wave (revised upward from **159** announced on 8 July), **42** for that of 4–17 July, and no excess mortality from 28 July to 16 August. Nationally, the total reaches approximately **2,935 excess deaths**. The file also documents variations in thresholds and rates across successive publications.

### Employment: Unemployment at 16.0% in August, Highest Since December 2020

Brussels unemployment reaches **16.0% in August 2026**, the highest level since December 2020, with **99,458 registered job seekers**, up **4.9% year-on-year**. The month records **8,887 entries** against **6,821 exits**. The most significant shift concerns status categories: **34,830 job seekers** (**35% of total**) are now registered with a CPAS, a rise of **82.6% year-on-year**. Actiris attributes this shift to time-limited unemployment benefits, which transfer the burden from federal to CPAS level.

### Mobility: Secured Bike Parking in High Demand

**14,263 requests** for secured bike parking remain pending across the Brussels Region, according to a parliamentary response of **2 September 2026**. By contrast, **4,700 spaces** have been created over four years, approximately **1,175 per year**. Schaerbeek leads with over **2,000 requests**, followed by Ixelles, Forest, and Saint-Gilles; **85% of requests** concern residential addresses. The government announces four initiatives, including making bike parking mandatory in planning permits.

### D'Ieteren Automotive: Up to 344 Jobs at Risk

D'Ieteren Automotive presented on **3 September 2026** to its works council a 2026–2030 restructuring plan threatening up to **344 jobs** out of **2,945** full-time equivalents (**11.7% of workforce**): approximately **236 salaried staff** and around one hundred manual workers. All seven Belgian sites are affected. The spokesperson places approximately **12% of threatened positions** in Brussels, where the Rue du Mail delivery centre in Ixelles would close. The Renault Act procedure was initiated the same day.

### Molenbeek-Saint-Jean: Council Fails to Reach Quorum

The municipal council was unable to sit twice in two weeks, for lack of a quorum. ### Emergency Shelter: Capacity Halved

From **1 September 2026**, the Brussels Deal emergency shelter scheme is reduced from **2,000 to 1,000 places**. Established end-2022 and funded at **forty-two million euros annually**, it is now halved following a two-stage decision and unsuccessful advocacy for delay. The first comparable data point will come from the ninth count of homeless persons on the night of 15–16 October 2026. The eighth count, on the night of 6–7 November 2024, had registered **9,777 people**, including **1,991 women** and **1,678 minors**.

### Data on Data Centres and Energy Efficiency

The future European data centre label will grade without penalising. The draft delegated regulation, revised on **2 July 2026**, sets no minimum threshold and no penalty for a poor grade: a site rated G remains compliant. The leverage lies in green finance, public procurement and the future European regulation on cloud and artificial intelligence. Two new features matter for Brussels: the water criterion now covers freshwater withdrawn rather than drinking water alone, and the label will display **five additional indicators** on top of PUE and WUE, including recognition of nuclear energy. First label on **15 August 2027**, valid for one year and renewed automatically.

Call for tenders in 2026, award in 2028, opening targeted for 2030, followed by thirty years of public-private partnership operation.
