> *<span lang="fr">« Non, on ne les sanctionne pas. »</span>*
>
> (No, we do not sanction them.)
>
> The Brussels Alderman for Economic Affairs in 2024, asked why fairground operators breaching noise rules were not sanctioned. BX1, 13 September 2024.

The Midi Fair (Foire du Midi, Zuidfoor) celebrates its **146th edition** in 2026, from **18 July to 23 August**, with about **130 rides** on the boulevard du Midi. It is one of the largest travelling funfairs in Europe, and a textbook case for Brussels governance: one municipality organises and collects, several neighbouring municipalities get the noise, and no public data makes it possible to say who pays for what.

## A municipal institution, not a regional one

Contrary to what its route along the edge of several municipalities might suggest, the Midi Fair is an **initiative of the City of Brussels alone**, led by its **Alderman for Economic Affairs**. The City's official website (bruxelles.be/foire-du-midi) and the fair's own website (foiredumidi.brussels) say so unambiguously. No source consulted mentions Anderlecht or Saint-Gilles as co-organisers, even though the boulevard du Midi runs along their administrative boundary. Porte d'Anderlecht appears only as a geographical landmark, not as a sign of shared competence.

Since **1 April 2024**, the regional authorisation to carry out a fairground activity in the Brussels Region has been abolished: municipal authorities now check that the conditions are met and allocate the pitches. Every Brussels municipality, including Anderlecht and Saint-Gilles, therefore has its own regulation on fairground activities, but that framework concerns **their own local fairs**, not the Midi Fair itself. Confusing the two would be a mistake: a general municipal regulatory competence does not make those municipalities co-organisers of this particular event.

In 2026, the City of Brussels Alderman for Economic Affairs is **Didier Wauters (Les Engagés)**, who presented the 145th edition (2025) alongside the president of the **fairground operators' committee**, **Patrick De Corte**. In 2024, the same portfolio was held by **Benhur Ergen**, which places the change of office holder after the municipal elections of October 2024.

## 1880 or 1885? A double origin

The sources do not agree on a single founding date, and the two dates most often cited actually refer to two different things. In **1880**, the Brussels municipal council decided to merge three separate fairs (Grand-Place, place des Martyrs, Marché-aux-Grains) into one large fair on the boulevard du Midi, to mark the **fiftieth anniversary of Belgian independence**. A joint committee of traders and municipal councillors was set up in **1882** to organise the event. Only from **1885**, after some hesitation, did it become a systematic annual event.

**1880 is therefore the year of the founding decision; 1885 marks the start of the annual event, interrupted at least in 2020.** Both dates are attested by the fair's official website, by Wikipedia (FR and EN) and by the ArchivIris archives; none of the sources consulted disputes this sequence.

**Correction (11 September 2026).** This dossier presented 1885 as the start of an unbroken annual tradition, whereas the 2020 edition was cancelled.

The municipal authorities of the City of Brussels then took over the organisation and began subsidising it, up to the present situation in which the fair is explicitly presented as « an initiative of the City of Brussels Alderman for Economic Affairs ».

## Who pays, who collects: the unanswered question

This is the weakest point of the dossier, and the one that would most deserve institutional attention. **No source consulted, official or press, publishes the scale of concession fees paid by fairground operators**, nor the breakdown of municipal costs (police, fire and emergency services, cleaning, infrastructure) incurred for the event. The only figure available is a one-off: about **EUR 500,000** in compensation paid to fairground operators when the 2020 edition was cancelled because of Covid.

This budgetary opacity is precisely what residents of the neighbouring municipalities have been denouncing since 2024 (see below): in their view, the City of Brussels collects the fees from fairground operators while leaving the neighbouring municipalities to absorb part of the nuisance, with no documented compensation or coordination mechanism. BGM found no source that confirms or refutes this perception: it is reported as such, with that caveat.

## The only confirmed disruption: the 2020 cancellation, then the 2021 relocation

**2020: full cancellation.** The 2020 edition was simply cancelled because of the Covid-19 pandemic, as the limit then in force (200 people) made the event « unworkable » according to the authorities. The City of Brussels paid about EUR 500,000 in compensation to fairground operators.

**2021: partial relocation, Metro 3 works.** The edition went ahead, but part of the route was moved towards Arts-et-Métiers and Porte d'Anderlecht because of the Metro 3 works, which exposed new residents to the noise of the fair. The permitted noise limit was raised that year from 80 to 95 decibels, prompting new complaints.

**2022: lower attendance.** With about 700,000 visitors against more than a million in 2019, attendance had not yet returned to its pre-crisis level. The press attributes this fall partly to a heatwave that year rather than to a lasting Covid effect.

**Interruptions during the two world wars: not confirmed.** Contrary to a widespread idea about historic Brussels fairs, no source consulted, whether official, encyclopaedic or press, documents any interruption of the Midi Fair during the First or Second World War. This point remains a **research gap**, not an established fact: it would need dedicated archival research (municipal council deliberations, older ArchivIris holdings) before any claim is made.

## Residents' anger: the missing coordination (12 September 2024)

On **12 September 2024**, a meeting was held between fairground operators, the Alderman for Economic Affairs (then Benhur Ergen) and residents from **Anderlecht, Molenbeek-Saint-Jean and Saint-Gilles**. The grievances raised:

- **Noise nuisance** above legal limits, with no sanctions against fairground operators in breach;
- **Cleanliness problems** linked to waste management;
- **A feeling of insecurity** in the neighbourhood for the duration of the fair;
- **No police coordination** between the City of Brussels and the neighbouring police zones and municipalities.

One resident summed up the situation: « cooperation with the [local] police zone simply does not exist. » Asked why fairground operators in breach were not sanctioned, the Alderman replied bluntly: « no, we do not sanction them. » In response, the City presented attendance figures and comparisons of noise levels with other cities, without announcing any concrete corrective measure.

**This point rests on a single press source** (BX1, 13 September 2024). BGM found no official City statement, no municipal council minutes and no second media outlet corroborating the details of that meeting. It is reported here as such, pending confirmation.

## The 2026 edition: the 146th, from 18 July to 23 August

The 2026 edition is officially confirmed by the City of Brussels and by the fair's own website: **146th edition**, from **Saturday 18 July** to **Sunday 23 August 2026**, about **130 rides** for all ages. Free entry to the whole site, with rides paid individually. This continuity follows the recent editions: the 144th in 2024 and the 145th in 2025 (19 July to 24 August, with more than a million visitors expected according to the organisers).

In July 2025, the City publicly stressed safety: stricter checks and a reminder to fairground operators that « we are not above the law ». No precise figure on the police or security resources deployed was made public, however.

The fair is also listed in the inventory of Brussels intangible heritage, as part of the wider recognition of Belgian and French fairground culture (a dossier carried jointly by Belgium and France).

## Caveats: what remains to be investigated

This version of the dossier relies on primary sources (official websites of the City of Brussels and of the fair, hub.brussels) and on verified press sources. Several grey areas remain open:

1. **Scale of concession fees paid by fairground operators.** No public document identified. Access to the budget deliberations of the City of Brussels municipal council would be needed.
2. **Detailed municipal costs** (police, fire and emergency services, cleaning, infrastructure): not published. No breakdown found in the budget documents consulted remotely.
3. **Interruptions during the two world wars**: not confirmed by any source. Dedicated archival research needed (ArchivIris, historic municipal council deliberations) before any claim is made.
4. **Residents' dispute of September 2024**: resting on a single press source (BX1), with no official corroboration or second media outlet identified so far.
5. **Follow-up after 2024**: no source documents any formal institutional response (audit, follow-up meeting, policy change) from the City of Brussels since the September 2024 meeting.
6. **Numbering of editions**: at least one inconsistency noted in the press for an earlier edition (2023, presented as the « 142nd » by one outlet and the « 143rd » by another). The « 146th » numbering for 2026 is sourced here to the official websites, not to the press.

These points do not invalidate the thrust of the dossier: they sharpen its outline and set the agenda for the next iteration.

## Why the Midi Fair matters for Brussels governance

The Midi Fair is not just a popular summer event. It is:

- a textbook case of **single-municipality governance on a shared territory**: one authority organises, collects and decides, while exposure to nuisance (noise, waste, safety) spills well over into the territory of other municipalities with no documented compensation mechanism;
- a revealing example of the **everyday budgetary opacity** of major Brussels municipal events: unlike the BIFFF (funding set out in an agreement and published after several years of media pressure), the Midi Fair publishes no figure on fees or costs, a hundred and forty-six years after it began;
- a test of **institutional resilience** in the face of external shocks: the 2020 Covid cancellation and the forced relocation of 2021 (Metro 3 works) show that an old, well-established event can absorb a complete interruption and return to full scale within a few years, without any governance reform following.

Following it means watching whether the residents' documented pressure since 2024 ever leads to budgetary transparency or formalised police coordination between the City of Brussels and the neighbouring municipalities, or whether the situation remains, as it is today, undocumented.
