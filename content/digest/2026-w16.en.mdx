## Citizens' Assemblies: three years of proposals, a decree still pending

On 4 March 2026, Brussels Environment published a three-year review of the Citizens' Assembly for the Climate. The process has generated more than **100 cumulative proposals** addressing habitat, food, and the sharing economy. Cycle 3, devoted to sharing, closed this week with a public opinion proposing **8 priorities**: visibility plan for initiatives, project incubator, adaptation of subsidies, federation of actors, library expansion, shared cars for youth, energy hubs, solar panels. However, the ministerial decree to enshrine this citizen initiative has still not been adopted as of 19 April, exceeding the original deadline of end-2025. The Ixelles Participatory Budget, in its 4th edition, held its juries on 8 and 9 April with a budget of **170 000 EUR**; final results are being finalized.

## Chief Architect: accelerating procedures and affordable housing

On 16 April 2026, the Brussels Government approved the strategic plan of Chief Architect Lisa De Visscher. Five axes structure this ambition: (1) procedure acceleration — the Chief Architect's opinion will be delivered in half the time for projects ≥ **5 000 m²**; (2) affordable housing through housing cooperatives and public-private partnerships, breaking the speculative cycle; (3) public space resilience — adaptation to heat, increased tree canopy, soil unsealing; (4) conversion of vacant buildings; (5) cross-cutting quality, continuity of architecture competitions and partner linkage.

## Employment: Actiris budget preserved, limited ACS reductions

On 16 April 2026, the Brussels Government agreed on an adapted 2026 Actiris budget, three weeks after rejection of the initial draft and the resignation of the Director General. The initial effort of **40 M EUR** in cuts is reduced to approximately **28 M EUR** (−12 M EUR). The retained concessions are substantial: ACS article 20 preserved, no 95% job standardization abandoned, Actiris partnership budget maintained in full at **67 M EUR**, First Employment Agreements (CPE) preserved in Brussels administrations. The consolidated ACS budget reaches **276 M EUR**. The suppression of the Activa.brussels bonus is confirmed, with replacement by a new mechanism in 2027. Florence Lepoivre (FGTB Brussels) described the Activa reform as « perhaps the least problematic measure ».

## Security: escalation of violence in Saint-Gilles, SIAMU relocated

Between 15 and 18 April, Saint-Gilles experienced three serious incidents: an explosion on rue de Bosnie (~10 vehicles damaged), gunfire on rue Gisbert Combaz (3 impacts, no casualties), an explosion at the OKLM Chicha bar on rue Théodore Verhaegen (facade destroyed, 15 windows of the Institut des Filles de Marie damaged). Mayor Jean Spinette (PS) described the situation as « mind-boggling » and a « gangrene », calling for stronger investigation capacity rather than a military deployment: « Brussels does not need soldiers, but investigators and patrols. »

Meanwhile, from 14 to 17 April, SIAMU moved to its new advanced post Delta in Ixelles: a **2 700 m²** facility on 5 floors (vs 800 m² at the old post) housing 5 trucks and 4 ambulances. Major innovation: a post-fire decontamination system unprecedented in Belgium, with two separate circuits reducing exposure to carcinogenic residues. Operational coverage was maintained without interruption.

The night-time closure ordinance (1am-6am) for the North Quarter, in force since early April, caused its first public incident on 14 April: the evacuation of **120 people** from the Royale Layalina nightclub in Saint-Josse, with the manager announcing a possible « short-term bankruptcy » and preparing summary proceedings.

## Mobility: Good Move succession, LEZ fines postponed to 1 July

Mobility Minister Elke Van den Brandt (Groen) set out on 14 and 15 April the timetable for Good Move's succession: a note to the government before summer 2026, evaluation finalised at the end of 2026, possible corrections in 2027-2030, a new plan from 2030. The STOP principle (pedestrians > cyclists > public transport > cars) is maintained. The minister was self-critical about participation: resistance in Cureghem and Schaerbeek was attributed to a lack of support and insufficient consultation. « If I have to remove parking spaces to make a space safer, I will. »

On the LEZ, Finance Minister Dirk De Smedt (Anders) announced on 17 April that fines are postponed to **1 July 2026** (initially planned for 7 June). The political agreement of 4 April remains the reference (pass **350 EUR**, social rate **200 EUR**, monthly fine **80 EUR**, exemptions taskforce before 15 June); only the timetable is adjusted, justified by administrative work « still in progress » and the adaptation of Brussels Taxation's systems.

## Economy: Hub.brussels closes 14 offices abroad

On 16 April 2026, the Brussels Government confirmed the closure of **14 of Hub.brussels' 33 offices** abroad. Offices closed: Milan, Barcelona, Copenhagen, Belgrade, Geneva (Europe); Abu Dhabi, Hanoi, Montevideo, San Francisco, Shanghai, Vancouver (rest of the world); Havana (previously announced). Decentralised management: The Hague, Lille, Luxembourg. Expected savings: **3 to 6 M EUR/year**. Around **30 employees** are affected. Minister-President Boris Dilliès (MR) stated: « Maintaining 33 offices abroad is a luxury Brussels cannot afford. »

## Social: 14 246 Brussels students live on an integration income

On 15 April 2026, data from the PPS Social Integration revealed that **14 246 adult students** pursue their studies on an integration income (RIS) in Brussels, more than in all of Flanders (**10 064**) despite a population five times smaller. Wallonia counts **18 421**. The RIS for a single person amounts to **1 340 EUR/month**. This data complements the Vivalis 2025 Social Barometer published the same day: monetary poverty at **23 %** in Brussels (vs 7 % in Flanders, 13 % in Wallonia), **47 304 RIS beneficiaries** on 1 January 2025, more than one adult in ten in Molenbeek and Saint-Josse. Six of the ten poorest municipalities in Belgium are in Brussels. On housing, the most deteriorated indicator: **55 572 households** on the waiting list (+78 % in 15 years), 24 % in unfit housing, 30 % overcrowded. The **poorest 2 %** spend > 50 % of their income on rent, leaving them about **10 EUR/person/day**. Health inequalities: a life expectancy gap of ~**5 years** between municipalities, diabetes **3× more frequent** among the poorest 20 %.

## City of Brussels: horeca taxes raised, Annie Cordy mural destroyed

The City of Brussels municipal council voted on 20 April an upward revision of taxes on horeca terraces. New rates: pedestrian zone **45 EUR/m²** for terraces > 50 m² (+50 %) and **35 EUR/m²** ≤ 50 m² (+46 %); Grand-Place and Sablon **50 EUR/m²** (+28 %); outside the pedestrian zone < 50 m² unchanged (**19 EUR/m²**). Multipliers: floor ×1.25, semi-enclosed ×3, enclosed ×10. Displayed menu: +200 EUR flat fee. The Brussels horeca federation « regrets » the measure, calling it a « bad signal » in a context marked by the federal increase in hotel VAT (6 % → 12 %, 1 March 2026).

During the night of 14 to 15 April, a fire devastated the barracks of the Annie Cordy park in Laeken. The mural created in 2018 for the artist's 90th birthday, which had become a symbol of the neighbourhood, was destroyed. The redevelopment of the park, already planned, includes a bust of Annie Cordy and a new mural by street artist Amandine Lesay (design approved at the end of 2021).

## BIFFF: 60 000 spectators, a record since the return to Heysel

The 44th edition of BIFFF (closing 18 April) attracted **60 000 spectators** (+30 %, +12 000 vs the previous edition's ~48 000), a record since the post-COVID return to Heysel. Re-engineering under constraint (a single venue, 16 days, 3 weekends) worked both as a survival strategy AND as a driver of audience growth. Awards: Golden Raven to Never After Dark (Dave Boyle, Japan).
