## FWB programme decree adopted at dawn on 5 June

During the night of 4 to 5 June, the Parliament of the Wallonia-Brussels Federation adopted the programme decree at **4:00 am** after fourteen hours of debate (MR-Engagés coalition in favour; PS, PTB and Ecolo against).

The main measures: the weekly teaching load of upper secondary teachers rises from **20 to 22 hours** without salary compensation, sick leave and end-of-career leave (DPPR) are tightened for statutory staff, and higher education enrolment fees will reach **EUR 1,194** for 58% of students from the next academic year. Taken together, the measures represent around **EUR 500 million** in savings by 2029.

The day before, the demonstration of **4 June** in Brussels had turned into an incident: intrusion into the FWB Parliament, a smoke bomb, firefighters forced to interrupt an intervention, around ten arrests. The inter-union strike notice remains valid until **10 July**, the last day of the school year. The unions are threatening the end-of-year exams.

## Investigation opened into police conduct during the demonstrations

On **6 June**, the spokesperson for the mayor of the City of Brussels announced the opening of an investigation into the conduct of certain police officers during the FWB demonstrations of 5 and 6 June. Footage circulating on social media shows officers displaying a banned symbol and making inappropriate remarks. **Fifteen judicial arrests** and one administrative arrest had taken place on Thursday 5 June. Tensions persisted on Saturday **7 June** around Brussels Central Station.

## Administrative reform: pillar 1 passes second reading

On **5 June**, the Brussels government approved the second reading of the first of the four pillars of the regional administrative restructuring. The reform aims to group the roughly **25 regional entities** into 4 large pillars by 2029, with a savings target of **EUR 250 to 300 million**. The file is being sent to the Brussels Parliament for approval. The first reading took place on 5 March 2026.

## LEZ: 13,000 vehicles affected on 1 July

The Brussels Region confirmed on **5 June** that more than **13,000 vehicles**, Euro 5 diesels and Euro 2 petrol cars, will be subject to LEZ fines of **EUR 350** from **1 July 2026**, following a ruling by the Constitutional Court. The cap remains one fine per quarter. Warning letters have been sent since 1 January 2026. The overall reform, with an annual pass at EUR 350 and a monthly fine at EUR 80, has not yet been finalised.

## Housing Fund: the regional guarantee is granted

On **4 June**, the Brussels government approved a regional guarantee of **EUR 100 million** on the 2026 borrowings of the Housing Fund of the Brussels-Capital Region. This guarantee secures the resumption on **1 July 2026** of mortgage loans for home purchase, suspended since summer 2025. The EUR 100 million ceiling corresponds to the cash impact expected in the fourth quarter.

## Non-profit sector: more than 18,000 demonstrators in Brussels

On **4 June**, the joint union front of the non-profit sector brought more than **18,000 people** together in Brussels (according to Medi-Sphere) to call for a federal social agreement and structural refinancing. The march went from Brussels Central Station to Place Poelaert. The joint committees concerned are the federal JCs **319, 330 and 331** (healthcare, welfare, socio-cultural). The sector denounces persistent budgetary uncertainty against a backdrop of growing pressure on federal public finances.
