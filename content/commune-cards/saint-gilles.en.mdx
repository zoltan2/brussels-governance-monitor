## A dense and vibrant municipality

Saint-Gilles is a dense municipality of nearly 50,000 inhabitants over 2.53 km², known for its cultural life and social diversity. The PS-Ecolo coalition was renewed for the 2024-2030 term. The PTB became the second political force (24.4%, 9 seats).

## Transparency: 3 out of 6 criteria

The **budget** is published online and the **mandate registry** is accessible via the transparency page. **Citizen participation** is active with the Citizen Budget Midi (EUR 300,000) and a permanent citizen council. However, no confirmed council **livestream**, **minutes** are only partially online, and no **Open Data** portal.

## Citizen participation: Citizen Budget Midi

Saint-Gilles stands out with its **Citizen Budget Midi** of EUR 300,000 via the 21solutions platform, garnering 6,738 votes. A **permanent citizen council** complements the participatory framework.

## Recent developments

The **Saint-Gilles prison**, whose closure had long been announced, will see its use **extended until 2035** under the federal Masterplan III penitentiary programme. On 3 March 2026, the **Water Bearer** statue (iconic fountain on the parvis) was removed for restoration.

## Escalation of drug-related violence (15-18 April 2026)

Three serious incidents hit Saint-Gilles over four nights, in the context of a **territorial war between criminal organisations** linked to drug trafficking.

| Night | Location | Event | Damage |
|-------|----------|-------|--------|
| 15 → 16 April (Wed-Thu) | rue de Bosnie | Explosion | ~10 vehicles damaged |
| 16 → 17 April (Thu-Fri) | rue Gisbert Combaz | Gunfire | 3 window impacts, no casualties |
| 17 → 18 April (Fri-Sat) | rue Théodore Verhaegen (OKLM Chicha bar, near the Barrière) | Explosion | Bar façade destroyed, 15 windows of Institut des Filles de Marie school shattered, no casualties |

**Reaction from Mayor Jean Spinette (PS)**: scene described as "hallucinatory", particularly after a school was directly hit. He speaks of "gangrene" for the municipality and calls for reinforced investigative capacity. Explicit stance: "Brussels does not need soldiers, but investigators and patrols to secure residents' nights."

**Police deployment**: reinforced presence announced day and night, mobilisation of the communal Prevention Service, additional federal resources. A meeting with affected residents is organised with police and the prevention service.

## Barrière de Saint-Gilles works: public transport chaos (June-July 2026)

A new phase of tram track renovation works on Avenue du Parc (Barrière de Saint-Gilles) began in late June 2026, causing major disruption: tram lines 81 and 97 are cut, replacement bus 96 is overcrowded. GUTIB (a public transport users' association) denounces a lack of coordination between the works. Mayor Jean Spinette formally requested a works coordination meeting and better communication to residents from regional Mobility Minister Elke Van den Brandt. The works phase runs until 23 August 2026, according to the official regional project.

## Urban planning: rue Émile Féron is transformed (15 July 2026)

An urban planning permit has been issued to redevelop rue Émile Féron, rue Joseph Claes and adjacent sections in the Midi neighbourhood, as part of the Sustainable Neighbourhood Contract Midi: planting of 270 trees, one-way traffic, traffic calming and rainwater management. Works are planned for spring 2027, over two years.

## Drug trafficking at Place Jacques Franck: two convictions

The Brussels criminal court sentenced two people for cocaine trafficking at Place Jacques Franck, to 12 months suspended imprisonment and a sentence of 20 months partly served, following an arrest on 2 December 2025 after police surveillance. This ruling follows on from the drug-related crime already documented in the municipality in April 2026.

## Childcare licence suspended for three months by the Flemish agency Opgroeien (25 July 2026)

The licence of the **Dream Land** childcare facility, located on communal territory, has been **suspended since 22 June 2026 for three months**.

**Which level of government?** The decision comes from the **Flemish agency Opgroeien** ("Growing Up"), competent for Dutch-speaking early-years childcare facilities, including those located in the Brussels-Capital Region. It comes **neither from the commune nor from the ONE** (the French Community's birth and childhood office). This is a direct illustration of the Brussels regime for person-related matters: on the same territory, childcare depends on the community authority the facility falls under.

**Grounds cited** — repeated deficiencies concerning:

- the ratio of **children present** to **carers**
- the **organisation of care**
- **quality assurance**
- the **pedagogical approach**
- **safety, health and hygiene**

**Scale.** The facility had a capacity of **16 places** but was caring for only **7 children**. According to the operator, **5 of the 7** found childcare elsewhere. The operator contests the decision.

Source: [VRT NWS (25 July 2026)](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/07/25/vergunning-van-kinderopvang-dream-land-in-sint-gillis-tijdelijk/). Confidence: unconfirmed (decision confirmed to the press by the Opgroeien agency; the suspension act itself was not consulted).

## Bloomberg Philanthropies: Saint-Gilles gives up funding for young people's climate projects (23 September 2026)

On **Wednesday 23 September 2026**, Saint-Gilles, Forest and Molenbeek-Saint-Jean, together with the city of Mons, announced that they were withdrawing from the **Youth Climate Action Fund** of the American foundation **Bloomberg Philanthropies**. At the end of August, these municipalities had been selected among the programme's winners, which was to pay **EUR 34,000** to each of them to help young people aged **15 to 24** carry out local climate projects.

The reason given is a report by the organisation Artists for Palestine UK, according to which Bloomberg Philanthropies funds the **Bloomberg-Sagol Center for City Leadership** at Tel Aviv University, which trains Israeli local leaders, including, according to BRUZZ, mayors of settlements in the occupied West Bank. After examining this report, the municipalities concluded that accepting the funding would not be compatible with their commitment to human rights and international law. Their joint statement reads: "Climate action is more urgent than ever, but it cannot come at the expense of Palestinians." According to BRUZZ, the announcement was relayed by the BDS (Boycott, Divestment, Sanctions) movement. According to The Brussels Times, cited by BRUZZ, Etterbeek is also among the beneficiary municipalities; none of the sources consulted reports a withdrawal by Etterbeek.
